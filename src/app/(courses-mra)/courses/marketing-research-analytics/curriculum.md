# Marketing Research & Analytics - Course Curriculum

**Subtitle**: Evidence for Decisions in Human and AI-Mediated Markets  
**Course Level**: Master's  
**Program**: Marketing  
**Duration**: 12 Weeks  
**Instructor**: Davood Wadi, PhD  
**Summary**: The design and conduct of marketing research in support of managerial decision-making: research design, measurement, sampling, experimentation, and multivariate and predictive analysis, with artificial intelligence as both an instrument and an object of research, including the evaluation of AI models and of the AI agents that shop on consumers' behalf.  
**Overview**: Marketing research produces evidence for managerial decisions, from the formulation of research problems, through qualitative, survey, and experimental designs, to statistical, multivariate, and predictive analysis. Artificial intelligence appears in two roles. As an instrument of research, it drafts, codes, simulates, and analyzes, and its output requires verification. As an object of research, AI models and the AI agents that search, evaluate, and purchase on consumers' behalf are held to the same standards of measurement, sampling, and experimental design that apply to human respondents.

---

## Weekly Breakdown

### Week 1: Research for Marketing Decisions
**Summary**: The role of marketing research in managerial decisions, from management decision problem to research problem, the research process, research ethics, AI in the research workflow, and AI agents as a subject of marketing research.

- The role of marketing research and analytics in the organization.
- From management decision problem to marketing research problem.
- The marketing research process, from problem definition to reporting.
- AI across the research workflow: what it speeds up, what it cannot replace, and research ethics.
- AI agents as a subject of marketing research: agent-mediated purchasing, and the evaluation of AI systems as a research task.

### Week 2: Research Design and Secondary Data
**Summary**: Exploratory, descriptive, and causal research designs, internal, syndicated, and public data sources, first-, second-, and third-party data, AI-assisted desk research, and audits and experiments on AI agents.

- Exploratory, descriptive, and causal research designs.
- Secondary data sources: internal, syndicated, and public data.
- First-party, second-party, and third-party customer data.
- AI-assisted desk research: summarizing sources, checking facts, and spotting fabricated references.
- Research designs for studying AI agents: descriptive audits of agent outputs and causal experiments on agent choice.

### Week 3: Qualitative Research
**Summary**: Depth interviews, focus groups, projective techniques, ethnography, qualitative coding and saturation, AI-moderated interviews and AI-assisted coding, and AI reasoning traces as verbal protocols.

- Depth interviews, focus groups, and projective techniques.
- Observation, ethnography, and netnography.
- Analyzing qualitative data: coding, themes, and saturation.
- AI-moderated interviews and AI-assisted coding, validated against human coders.
- Reasoning traces as verbal protocols: coding what AI agents state before they choose, and the limits of this evidence.

### Week 4: Measurement and Questionnaire Design
**Summary**: Levels of measurement, scaling techniques, reliability and validity, question wording, order effects, and response bias, AI-drafted survey items, and validated scales administered to AI models.

- Levels of measurement: nominal, ordinal, interval, and ratio.
- Scaling techniques: Likert, semantic differential, and rank-order scales.
- Reliability and validity of measures.
- Question wording, order effects, and response bias.
- Drafting and pretesting survey items with AI.
- Measuring the attitudes of AI models: administering validated scales to models, and response distributions in place of single answers.

### Week 5: Sampling and Data Collection
**Summary**: Populations, sampling frames, and sampling error, probability and non-probability sampling, sample size, online panel data quality, synthetic respondents, and sampling in AI evaluation.

- Populations, sampling frames, and sampling error.
- Probability and non-probability sampling. Determining sample size.
- Online panels and data quality: speeders, straight-liners, bots, and attention checks.
- Synthetic respondents: using AI to simulate consumers, and the limits of silicon samples.
- Sampling in AI evaluation: why a single model response is not a measurement, and how many responses a reliable estimate requires.

### Week 6: Data Preparation and Descriptive Analysis
**Summary**: Data cleaning, coding, and missing values, descriptive statistics, cross-tabulation, and visualization, the accuracy of AI data assistants, and the distribution of AI model responses.

- Data cleaning, coding, and handling missing values.
- Frequencies, measures of central tendency, and dispersion.
- Cross-tabulation and data visualization for decision makers.
- AI data-analysis assistants: asking questions of data and verifying the answers.
- Describing model behavior: distributions, consensus, and variability of responses across repeated samples and across models.

### Week 7: Hypothesis Testing and Group Differences
**Summary**: Statistical inference, t-tests, analysis of variance, and chi-square tests, statistical versus practical significance, p-hacking, multiple comparisons, and reproducibility, and tests comparing AI models and prompts.

- The logic of hypothesis testing: null and alternative hypotheses, p-values, and errors.
- Comparing groups: t-tests, ANOVA, and chi-square tests.
- Statistical significance versus practical significance and effect size.
- Research integrity: p-hacking, multiple comparisons, and reproducible analysis.
- Hypothesis testing in AI evaluation: comparing models and prompts, and the reliability of claims about model behavior.

### Week 8: Experiments and A/B Testing
**Summary**: Causal inference, laboratory, field, and online experiments, internal and external validity, quasi-experiments, simulated AI subjects, adaptive testing, and experiments on AI agents.

- Causality and the conditions for inferring it.
- Laboratory and field experiments. Internal and external validity.
- A/B and multivariate testing in digital marketing.
- Quasi-experiments when randomization is not possible.
- AI in experimentation: piloting designs with AI agents as simulated subjects, and adaptive testing with multi-armed bandits.
- Controlled experiments on AI agents: manipulating prompts, roles, and information environments in factorial designs.
- Process tracing with AI agents: separating failures of information acquisition from failures of information use.

### Week 9: Regression and Marketing Response Models
**Summary**: Linear and logistic regression as marketing response models, model fit and multicollinearity, marketing mix modeling and attribution, explanation versus prediction, and choice models of AI agents.

- Correlation and simple and multiple linear regression.
- Interpreting coefficients, fit, and multicollinearity.
- Logistic regression for choice and conversion outcomes.
- Marketing mix modeling and attribution.
- Explaining versus predicting: when machine learning outperforms regression and what it hides.
- Choice models for AI agents: estimating how agents weight attributes against a normative benchmark, and mediation analysis of agent choice.

### Week 10: Segmentation, Positioning, and Preference Analysis
**Summary**: Factor analysis, perceptual mapping, cluster analysis, and conjoint analysis for segmentation, positioning, and willingness to pay, embedding-based segmentation, AI personas, and the attribute trade-offs of AI agents.

- Factor analysis and perceptual mapping.
- Cluster analysis for market segmentation.
- Conjoint analysis and measuring willingness to pay.
- AI-driven segmentation: clustering customers with embeddings and turning segments into personas.
- Conjoint designs for AI agents: measuring the part-worths that agents assign to attributes such as price and sponsorship.

### Week 11: Customer Analytics and Predictive Modeling
**Summary**: Customer lifetime value, RFM analysis, churn and response prediction with tree-based and ensemble models, algorithmic bias, fairness, and explainability, and bias audits of language models and AI agents.

- Customer lifetime value (CLV) and RFM analysis.
- Churn and response prediction with decision trees and ensemble models.
- Evaluating predictive models: holdout samples, confusion matrices, and lift.
- Personalization and recommendation systems.
- Algorithmic bias, fairness, and explaining model decisions.
- Auditing language models and AI agents: behavioral evaluation beyond benchmarks, and position, sponsorship, and country-of-origin biases.

### Week 12: Text Analytics, AI-Driven Insights, and Reporting
**Summary**: Sentiment analysis, topic modeling, and classification of reviews and social media with large language models, privacy and data protection, reporting findings, and agent-mediated markets as a research agenda.

- Social listening and online reviews as research data.
- Sentiment analysis, topic modeling, and text classification with large language models.
- Privacy, consent, and data protection in marketing research.
- Communicating findings: the research report, data storytelling, and recommendations.
- The future of the insights function in an AI-enabled organization.
- Agent-mediated markets as a research agenda: how AI agents choose, whom they serve, and how brands appear to them.
