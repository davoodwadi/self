## Title Slide
- Week 4
- Business-to-business marketing addresses how organizations purchase goods and services through multi-role buying centers, structured procurement stages, and long-term key account partnerships that increasingly incorporate automated AI sourcing agents.

## Scope and Economic Scale of B2B Markets [no-exercise]
- Business buyer behavior refers to the purchasing behavior of organizations that acquire goods and services for use in the production of other products and services that are sold, rented, or supplied to others.
- It also includes the behavior of retailing and wholesaling firms that acquire goods for the purpose of reselling or renting them to others at a profit.
- Although consumer brands are more visible to the general public, total dollar volume in B2B transactions far exceeds consumer market volume because many raw-material, component, and wholesale transactions occur upstream of a single retail sale.
- Producing and selling a single pair of running shoes to a consumer requires prior B2B transactions for synthetic textiles, molded rubber soles, factory machinery, packaging cartons, freight logistics, and retail display fixtures.

## Categories of Organizational Buyers [no-exercise]
- Organizational markets comprise three distinct institutional categories: commercial enterprises, institutional buyers, and government bodies.
- Commercial enterprises purchase raw materials, manufactured components, capital equipment, and business services to produce their own commercial outputs or run daily operations.
- Institutional markets consist of schools, hospitals, nursing homes, and non-profit organizations that provide goods and services to people in their care, often operating under strict budgets and captive patronage.
- Government markets consist of federal, provincial or state, and municipal agencies that purchase or rent goods for public functions, typically requiring public tenders, open bidding, and strict regulatory compliance.

## Part 1: Characteristics of Business Markets [no-exercise]
- Business markets differ systematically from consumer markets in market structure, demand behavior, buying unit composition, and decision complexity.
- Recognizing these structural differences explains why B2B marketing programs emphasize technical documentation, total cost of ownership, and consultative selling.

## Market Structure and Derived Demand [exercise]
[figure: comparison of B2B and B2C market structure showing derived demand flowing upstream from household retail purchases to component and raw-material suppliers]
- A business marketer normally deals with far fewer but much larger buyers than a consumer marketer does, and those buyers are often geographically concentrated in industrial clusters.
- Business demand is derived demand: it ultimately derives from the demand for consumer goods and services downstream.
- If household demand for residential solar panels declines, commercial demand for photovoltaic silicon wafers, aluminum mounting racks, and inverter microchips declines proportionally.
- Because derived demand originates with end consumers, B2B marketers sometimes advertise directly to final consumers (as Intel did with "Intel Inside") to pull demand through the supply chain.

## Inelastic and Fluctuating Demand [no-exercise]
- Many business markets exhibit price-inelastic demand in the short run, meaning total industry demand for a component responds weakly to moderate price changes.
- An appliance manufacturer will not double its purchases of steel hinges simply because hinge prices fall by ten percent, nor can it immediately redesign its washing machines if hinge prices rise.
- At the same time, demand for any single supplier's hinges remains highly price-elastic if competing suppliers offer identical specifications.
- Business markets also experience fluctuating demand (the acceleration principle), in which a ten percent rise in consumer retail demand can cause a much larger surge in orders for new factory machinery and tooling.

## Nature of the Buying Unit and Decision Complexity [no-exercise]
- Compared with consumer purchases, a business purchase usually involves more decision participants and a more professional, formalized purchasing effort.
- Major B2B purchases are governed by committees of technical experts, financial analysts, and senior executives who evaluate detailed economic and engineering justifications.
- Because purchasing decisions are complex and high in financial and operational risk, the buying cycle from initial inquiry to signed contract can span months or years.
- In many B2B industries, buyers and sellers become closely interdependent, co-developing custom product specifications and integrating inventory systems.

## Part 2: Buying Situations and the Organizational Buying Center [no-exercise]
- Organizational purchases vary in complexity depending on whether the firm is repeating a routine order, modifying an existing contract, or buying a solution for the first time.
- Across these buying situations, decision authority is distributed across the roles of the organizational buying center.

## Three Types of Business Buying Situations [exercise]
[figure: continuum of business buying situations from straight rebuy through modified rebuy to new task across decision complexity and participant involvement]
- In a straight rebuy, the buyer routinely reorders something without any modifications, such as replenishing office paper, standard fasteners, or maintenance lubricants from an approved supplier list.
- In a modified rebuy, the buyer wants to modify product specifications, prices, delivery terms, or suppliers, giving "in" suppliers an incentive to protect the account and "out" suppliers an opening to pitch a better offer.
- In a new task situation, a company buys a product or service for the first time, such as adopting a cloud enterprise resource planning system or building a new assembly line.
- The greater the cost and risk of the new task, the larger the number of decision participants and the more extensive their information search.
- Many new-task buyers prefer systems selling (or solutions selling), purchasing a complete turnkey package of interlocked products and services from a single prime contractor rather than assembling separate parts.

## Roles Within the Organizational Buying Center [exercise]
[figure: five roles within the organizational buying center: users, influencers, buyers, deciders, and gatekeepers]
- The decision-making unit of a buying organization is called its buying center: all the individuals and units that play a role in the business purchase decision-making process.
- Users are the members of the organization who will actually use the purchased product or service, often initiating the buying proposal and helping define functional specifications.
- Influencers affect the buying decision by helping define technical specifications and providing information for evaluating alternatives, such as in-house engineers or external IT consultants.
- Buyers have formal organizational authority to select the supplier and arrange terms of purchase, although they may be overridden by senior management on major capital expenditures.

## Deciders, Gatekeepers, and Dynamic Membership [no-exercise]
- Deciders have formal or informal power to select or approve the final suppliers, often remaining behind the scenes in routine purchases while stepping forward in high-stakes contracts.
- Gatekeepers control the flow of information to others in the buying center, such as procurement assistants who screen vendor calls or technical staff who choose which supplier brochures reach department heads.
- The buying center is not a fixed and formally identified unit on the organizational chart: its membership and size shift with each product category and buying situation.
- A single individual may occupy multiple roles in a small firm (acting as user, buyer, and decider), whereas a large corporation may assign dozens of specialists to a single role.

## Discussion: Mapping the Buying Center for a Hospital Equipment Purchase

Identify which hospital personnel occupy the user, influencer, buyer, decider, and gatekeeper roles, and explain how the value proposition and evidence presented to the chief radiologist should differ from the proposal presented to the chief financial officer.

## Part 3: The Business Buying Decision Process [no-exercise]
- Business buyers move through an eight-stage procurement process when confronting a new-task buying situation, while skipping or automating stages in modified or straight rebuys.
- Understanding how specifications are written and how proposals are scored determines whether a supplier wins the contract.

## Problem Recognition to Product Specification [exercise]
- Stage 1 is problem recognition, which occurs when someone in the company recognizes a problem or need that can be met by acquiring a specific good or service, triggered internally (a machine breakdown or new product launch) or externally (a trade show or sales call).
- Stage 2 is general need description, in which the buyer prepares a summary describing the general characteristics and quantity of the needed item, such as durability, reliability, and output capacity.
- Stage 3 is product specification, in which the buying organization decides on and specifies the best technical product characteristics for a needed item with the help of an engineering team.
- Engineering teams frequently apply value analysis, an approach to cost reduction in which components are studied carefully to determine whether they can be redesigned, standardized, or made by less costly methods of production.

## Supplier Search to Supplier Selection [no-exercise]
- Stage 4 is supplier search, in which the buyer conducts a search to find the best vendors by reviewing trade directories, querying digital procurement portals, or requesting referrals from other firms.
- Stage 5 is proposal solicitation, in which the buyer invites qualified suppliers to submit formal written proposals or deliver detailed technical and financial presentations.
- Stage 6 is supplier selection, in which the buying center reviews proposals and selects one or more suppliers using a structured vendor scorecard.
- Buying centers score candidate suppliers on product and service quality, on-time delivery record, ethical corporate behavior, honest communication, competitive total lifecycle cost, and repair and maintenance capability.

## Order-Routine Specification and Performance Review [no-exercise]
- Stage 7 is order-routine specification, in which the buyer writes the final order with the chosen supplier, listing technical specifications, quantity needed, expected delivery schedule, return policies, and warranties.
- For recurring maintenance, repair, and operating items, buyers increasingly replace individual purchase orders with blanket contracts or vendor-managed inventory, in which the supplier monitors stock and replenishes automatically.
- Stage 8 is performance review, in which the buyer rates its satisfaction with the supplier's performance by polling internal users and auditing delivery and defect metrics.
- Based on the performance review, the buying center decides whether to continue, modify, or drop the supplier arrangement.

## Part 4: Relationship Marketing and Key Account Management [no-exercise]
- Because B2B markets feature concentrated buyer bases and high switching costs, retaining and growing existing accounts is usually far more profitable than chasing one-off transactions.
- Relationship marketing and Key Account Management (KAM) structure how suppliers partner with their most strategic business customers.

## Transaction Orientation Versus Relationship Marketing [no-exercise]
- Transactional marketing treats each order as an isolated negotiation in which the buyer and seller attempt to capture maximum immediate surplus at the other's expense.
- Relationship marketing focuses on building long-term, mutually profitable partnerships with key customers, suppliers, and distributors by delivering consistent value over time.
- In B2B relationship marketing, trust, reliability, and operational integration act as strong competitive moats that prevent rivals from poaching accounts with minor price discounts.
- Suppliers calculate customer lifetime value at the organizational level, recognizing that a five-year enterprise contract generates recurring maintenance, upgrade, and expansion revenues.

## Key Account Management (KAM) [exercise]
[figure: key account management progression from transactional vendor to preferred supplier and integrated strategic partner]
- In many B2B firms, a small fraction of business customers (often following the Pareto pattern in which roughly 20 percent of accounts generate 80 percent of revenue) accounts for the majority of sales and profit.
- Key Account Management (KAM) is the systematic process of assigning dedicated cross-functional teams to build deep, long-term relationships with the company's most valuable organizational customers.
- Rather than pitching off-the-shelf catalog items, a key account team embeds engineers, logistics specialists, and account directors to co-design customized solutions and align supply schedules.
- Dedicated key account programs coordinate service across all global branches of the client organization through a single point of accountability.

## Benefits and Risks of Strategic B2B Partnerships [no-exercise]
- Collaborative B2B partnerships generate substantial mutual benefits: higher account retention, joint product innovation, shared demand forecasting, and lower inventory holding costs across the supply chain.
- Satisfied key accounts also provide credible industry references and case studies that help the supplier win new business clients.
- At the same time, over-reliance on a single dominant key account exposes a supplier to severe concentration risk if that client demands steep price concessions, experiences financial distress, or switches to a competitor.
- Prudent B2B suppliers balance deep investment in current key accounts with ongoing business development to diversify their client portfolio.

## Part 5: AI Agents in Organizational Buying [no-exercise]
- Organizations are deploying AI procurement agents and e-procurement systems to automate routine reordering, screen vendor catalogs, and compare supplier bids.
- Examining which buying center roles AI agents assume shows how B2B suppliers must adapt both their digital data and their human relationship strategies.

## E-Procurement and Digital B2B Marketplaces [no-exercise]
- E-procurement refers to purchasing through electronic connections between buyers and sellers, including reverse auctions, trading exchanges, company buying sites, and extranet links.
- Enterprise resource planning (ERP) and e-procurement portals connect a manufacturer's production schedule directly to approved suppliers' inventory systems, cutting order-processing costs and fulfillment lead times.
- While early e-procurement systems followed rigid software rules to reorder identical part numbers, modern procurement platforms embed large language models that can read unstructured requests, vendor proposals, and compliance certificates.
- Digital procurement frees human purchasing officers from routine paperwork so they can focus on strategic supplier negotiations and complex new-task projects.

## Automated Sourcing and AI Roles in the Buying Center [exercise]
[figure: organizational buying center showing AI agents acting as buyer in straight rebuys, gatekeeper in vendor screening, and influencer in specification comparison while human executives remain deciders]
- As enterprises integrate large language models into their procurement software, AI agents take on distinct roles inside the organizational buying center depending on the buying situation.
- In a straight rebuy, an automated procurement agent monitors inventory levels and reorders standard maintenance and operating supplies automatically within pre-approved contract rules, acting directly as the buyer.
- In supplier search and proposal screening, an AI agent that parses hundreds of vendor catalogs and passes only a compliant shortlist to human procurement officers acts as a gatekeeper.
- When an AI agent compares complex technical specification sheets, calculates total cost of ownership across bids, and scores vendor reliability, it acts as an influencer shaping the buying center's evaluation.
- In high-risk modified rebuys and new-task purchases, human executives and committee members remain the final deciders because strategic, legal, and operational accountability rests with people.

## Marketing to Both Procurement Agents and Human Decision Makers [exercise]
- When organizational buyers use AI agents as gatekeepers and influencers, a B2B supplier can be eliminated before a human account executive ever gets a meeting.
- To pass automated screening, B2B suppliers must publish machine-readable technical specifications, transparent volume pricing tiers, verified compliance certifications (such as ISO or SOC 2 standards), and on-time delivery metrics.
- Vague corporate brochures or unindexed PDF catalogs that require a phone call to obtain basic compatibility facts risk being skipped by an AI sourcing agent under time or lookup constraints.
- Once the supplier enters the shortlist, human salespeople and key account managers remain indispensable for building interpersonal trust, negotiating custom contract terms, and guiding complex organizational change.

## Discussion: Adapting B2B Sales Strategy for AI Procurement Gatekeepers
- Discussion: A mid-sized industrial sensor manufacturer has historically relied on trade shows and personal relationships with factory floor engineers to win contracts, keeping its technical tolerances and volume pricing confidential until a sales representative visits the client. Now, major automotive clients use AI procurement agents to screen global supplier databases and generate a three-vendor shortlist before inviting bids. How must the manufacturer restructure its digital catalog, pricing transparency, and sales force workflow to survive automated gatekeeping while protecting its key account relationships?

## Key Takeaways
- B2B marketing encompasses commercial enterprises, institutions, and government bodies whose total transaction volume exceeds B2C retail volume because multiple industrial exchanges precede every finished consumer product.
- Compared with consumer markets, business markets feature fewer and larger buyers, derived demand that originates in consumer demand, short-run price inelasticity, and fluctuating demand driven by the acceleration effect.
- Business purchases divide into straight rebuys, modified rebuys, and new-task situations, with systems selling addressing complex turnkey requirements.
- The organizational buying center is a dynamic decision unit comprising five roles: users, influencers, buyers, deciders, and gatekeepers.
- New-task business buying progresses through eight procurement stages from problem recognition and value-analysis product specification to proposal solicitation, supplier selection, and performance review.
- Relationship marketing and Key Account Management (KAM) shift B2B strategy from isolated transactions to long-term cross-functional partnerships that maximize organizational lifetime value.
- AI procurement agents now act as buyers in straight rebuys, gatekeepers in supplier screening, and influencers in bid comparison, requiring B2B suppliers to pair machine-readable technical and pricing disclosures with high-trust human key account management.
