## Title Slide
- Week 10
- Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) coordinates the five tools of the promotion mix to deliver a clear, consistent, and persuasive brand message across traditional, digital, and generative AI channels.

## The Promotion Mix and Customer Communication [no-exercise]
- Modern marketing calls for more than developing a good product, pricing it attractively, and making it accessible to target customers: companies must also communicate their value propositions clearly and persuasively.
- A company's total promotion mix (also called its marketing communications mix) consists of the specific blend of advertising, public relations, personal selling, sales promotion, and direct and digital marketing tools that the company uses to persuasively communicate customer value and build customer relationships.
- Beyond these planned promotional tools, every element of the marketing mix communicates something to the buyer: the product's styling, its price, the shape and color of its package, and the stores that sell it all send brand signals.
- To achieve maximum communication impact, the entire marketing mix must be coordinated so every customer contact reinforces the same core message.

## Why Media Fragmentation Demands Integrated Marketing Communications [no-exercise]
[figure: Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) model coordinating advertising, personal selling, sales promotion, public relations, and direct and digital marketing around a consistent brand message]
- Over the past several decades, mass-market broadcasting has given way to a fragmented landscape of hundreds of streaming services, social feeds, podcasts, search engines, mobile apps, and AI assistants.
- In this fragmented environment, consumers are bombarded by commercial messages from many organizational departments: an advertising agency creates television and video spots, a public relations firm handles press releases, a sales promotion specialist designs digital coupons, and an e-commerce team manages website banners.
- When these functional specialists operate in silos, conflicting messages can result in confused company images, inconsistent brand positions, and wasted promotional spending.
- Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) solves this problem by carefully integrating and coordinating the company's many communications channels to deliver a clear, consistent, and compelling message about the organization and its products.

## Part 1: The Five Tools of the Promotion Mix [no-exercise]
- Each of the five major promotion tools has unique characteristics, reach profiles, and cost structures.
- Marketers must understand the strengths and limitations of each tool in order to blend them effectively for a given product and target market.

## Advertising and Public Relations [exercise]
- Advertising is any paid form of nonpersonal presentation and promotion of ideas, goods, or services by an identified sponsor, using broadcast, print, online, mobile, or outdoor media.
- Advertising can reach masses of geographically dispersed buyers at a low cost per exposure, and it enables the seller to repeat a message many times while signaling brand scale and legitimacy, though it is impersonal and cannot persuade as directly as a company salesperson.
- Public relations (PR) involves building good relations with the company's various publics by obtaining favorable publicity, building up a good corporate image, and handling or heading off unfavorable rumors, stories, and events.
- Public relations is highly believable because news stories, features, sponsorships, and events seem more authentic and credible to readers than paid advertisements, and PR can reach prospects who actively avoid salespeople and ads.

## Personal Selling and Sales Promotion [exercise]
- Personal selling is the personal presentation by the firm's sales force for the purpose of engaging customers, making sales, and building customer relationships.
- It is the most effective tool at certain stages of the buying process, particularly in building up buyers' preferences, convictions, and actions in complex B2B or high-ticket consumer purchases, though a sales force requires a longer-term financial commitment than advertising.
- Sales promotion consists of short-term incentives to encourage the purchase or sale of a product or service, including consumer promotions (coupons, cash rebates, cents-off deals, contests, and point-of-purchase displays) and trade promotions (discounts and allowances to retailers).
- Whereas advertising says "buy our product," sales promotion says "buy it now": it attracts immediate attention and rewards quick buyer response, but its effects are often short-lived and less effective than advertising or personal selling in building long-run brand preference.

## Direct and Digital Marketing and Push Versus Pull Strategies [exercise]
[figure: comparison of a push promotion strategy (producer promoting to resellers who promote to consumers) and a pull promotion strategy (producer promoting directly to consumers who demand the product from resellers)]
- Direct and digital marketing involves engaging directly with carefully targeted individual consumers and customer communities to both obtain an immediate response and build lasting customer relationships, using email, social media, search ads, mobile apps, and catalogs.
- It is more targeted, immediate, and personalized than mass media, and it is interactive, allowing a two-way dialogue between the marketing team and the customer.
- Across all five tools, marketers can choose between two basic promotion mix strategies: a push strategy or a pull strategy.
- A push strategy involves "pushing" the product through marketing channels to final consumers using the sales force and trade promotions to induce channel members to carry and promote the product, whereas a pull strategy spends heavily on consumer advertising and promotion to induce final consumers to ask retailers for the product, pulling it through the channel.

## Part 2: How Communication Works and Steps in Developing IMC [no-exercise]
- Effective marketing communications rest on a clear model of how messages travel from a sender to a receiver and how noise can distort decoding.
- Building an IMC campaign follows a six-step sequence from identifying the target audience to collecting feedback.

## The Communication Process Model [no-exercise]
[figure: nine-element communication model showing sender, encoding, message, media, decoding, receiver, response, feedback, and noise]
- Integrated marketing communications involves identifying the target audience and shaping a well-coordinated promotional program to obtain the desired audience response, analyzed through the nine elements of the communication process.
- Two elements represent the major parties in a communication (the sender and the receiver), and two represent the major communication tools (the message and the media).
- Four elements represent major communication functions: encoding (putting thought into symbolic form), decoding (the process by which the receiver assigns meaning to the symbols encoded by the sender), response (the reactions of the receiver after being exposed to the message), and feedback (the part of the receiver's response communicated back to the sender).
- The final element is noise, the unplanned static or distortion during the communication process that results in the receiver getting a different message than the one the sender sent, requiring senders to encode messages in symbols that match the receiver's field of experience.

## Identifying the Target Audience and Buyer-Readiness Stages [exercise]
[figure: six buyer-readiness stages progressing from awareness and knowledge through liking, preference, and conviction to purchase]
- A marketing communicator starts with a clear target audience in mind, whether potential buyers or current users, those who make the buying decision or those who influence it, and individuals or specific groups.
- Once the target audience has been defined, the marketer determines the desired communication objectives, which in most cases seek a purchase response that results from a long consumer decision-making process.
- The marketing communicator needs to know where the target audience now stands and to what stage it needs to be moved across six buyer-readiness stages: awareness, knowledge, liking, preference, conviction, and purchase.
- Consistent with the AIDA framework, the message must get Attention, hold Interest, arouse Desire, and obtain Action: awareness and knowledge advertising cannot by itself close sales if target buyers lack conviction or face friction at checkout.

## Designing the Message: Content, Structure, Format, and Source [exercise]
- Having defined the desired audience response, the communicator turns to developing an effective message by resolving three message problems (message content, message structure, and message format) and selecting a credible message source.
- Message content requires figuring out an appeal or theme that will produce the desired response across three types of appeals: rational appeals (relating to the audience's self-interest in quality, economy, value, or performance), emotional appeals (stirring up negative or positive emotions that motivate a purchase), and moral appeals (directed to the audience's sense of what is right and proper).
- Message structure decisions address three issues: whether to draw a definite conclusion or leave it to the audience, whether to present a one-sided argument (mentioning only strengths) or a two-sided argument (touting strengths while also admitting a minor shortcoming), and whether to present the strongest arguments first or last.
- Message format requires a strong layout, headline, illustration, color, voice, and body language, while the message source's impact depends on the communicator's expertise and trustworthiness.

## Discussion: Designing a Push-and-Pull Promotion Mix for a Medical Device vs. a Consumer App
- Discussion: A health-technology company is launching two products simultaneously: a 15,000-dollar clinical rehabilitation device sold to physical therapy clinics and a 9-dollar-per-month consumer posture-coaching mobile app. Using the five promotion mix tools, push versus pull strategy, and the six buyer-readiness stages, compare how the company should allocate its promotional effort for each product and explain which message appeals (rational, emotional, or moral) fit each audience.

## Part 3: Selecting Media Channels [no-exercise]
- The communicator must now select the channels of communication through which the message will reach the target audience.
- Media channels divide broadly into personal and nonpersonal communication channels, requiring media planners to balance reach, frequency, impact, and engagement.

## Personal Communication Channels and Word-of-Mouth Influence [no-exercise]
- In personal communication channels, two or more people communicate directly with each other, whether face to face, on the phone, via mail or email, or through an internet chat or video call.
- Some personal communication channels are controlled directly by the company (such as company salespeople contacting business buyers), whereas others reach buyers through independent neighbors, friends, family members, associates, or online reviewers talking to target buyers, known as word-of-mouth influence.
- Personal influence carries great weight, especially for products that are expensive, risky, or highly visible, because buyers trust peers more than paid commercial claims.
- Companies use buzz marketing and opinion-leader outreach by cultivating industry experts, community leaders, and credible creators to experience the product and share authentic word of mouth with their networks.

## Nonpersonal Communication Channels and Media Planning [no-exercise]
- Nonpersonal communication channels are media that carry messages without personal contact or feedback, including major media (print, broadcast, display, and online media), atmospheres (designed environments that create or reinforce the buyer's leaning toward buying a product, such as a bank lobby or luxury boutique), and events (staged occurrences that communicate messages to target audiences, such as press conferences or grand openings).
- Selecting specific media for advertising requires deciding on the reach, frequency, and impact needed to achieve the campaign's objectives.
- Reach is a measure of the percentage of people in the target market who are exposed to the ad campaign during a given period of time, frequency is a measure of how many times the average person in the target market is exposed to the message, and media impact is the qualitative value of a message exposure through a given medium.
- Media planners also choose among major media types based on target audience media habits, nature of the product, types of messages, and cost per thousand exposures, and schedule ads continuously or in pulses over the year.

## Collecting Feedback and Evaluating Communication Effects [no-exercise]
- After sending the message, the communicator must research its effect on the target audience to close the feedback loop of the communication process.
- This involves asking target audience members whether they remember the message, how many times they saw it, what points they recall, how they felt about the message, and their past and present attitudes toward the product and company.
- The communicator would also like to measure behavior resulting from the message, such as how many people bought the product, talked to others about it, or visited the store or website.
- Feedback on marketing communications may suggest changes in the promotion program or in the product offer itself: if awareness is high but trial is low, the product's price or distribution rather than the ad may need adjustment.

## Part 4: Setting the Promotional Budget and Measuring ROI [no-exercise]
- One of the hardest marketing decisions facing a company is how much to spend on promotion, with spending ratios varying widely from a few percent of sales in industrial commodities to 30 percent or more in cosmetics and beverages.
- Companies use four common methods to set the total budget for advertising and promotion, and evaluate campaigns through communication and sales returns.

## Four Methods for Setting the Total Promotion Budget [exercise]
- Industries and companies use four common methods to set the total budget for promotion: the affordable method, the percentage-of-sales method, the competitive-parity method, and the objective-and-task method.
- The affordable method sets the promotion budget at the level management thinks the company can afford, which completely ignores the effects of promotion on sales and leads to an uncertain annual promotion budget that makes long-range planning difficult.
- The percentage-of-sales method sets the promotion budget at a certain percentage of current or forecasted sales (or a percentage of the unit sales price), which is simple to calculate but wrongly views sales as the cause of promotion rather than as the result, leading firms to cut promotion precisely when sales drop and support is needed most.
- The competitive-parity method sets the promotion budget to match competitors' outlays, whereas the most logical budget-setting method is the objective-and-task method, whereby the company sets its promotion budget based on what it wants to accomplish with promotion by (1) defining specific promotion objectives, (2) determining the tasks needed to achieve these objectives, and (3) estimating the costs of performing these tasks.

## Measuring Promotional Effectiveness and Return on Investment [no-exercise]
- Marketing management must continually evaluate the return on promotion investment by measuring both the communication effects and the sales and profit effects of the campaign.
- Measuring communication effects (or copy testing) can be done before or after an ad is printed, broadcast, or posted online: before the ad is placed, the advertiser can show it to consumers, ask how they like it, and measure message recall or attitude changes, and after the ad is run, the advertiser can measure how the ad affected consumer recall or product awareness, knowledge, and preference.
- Sales and profit effects of promotion are often harder to measure than communication effects because sales are influenced by many factors besides promotion, such as product features, price, availability, macroeconomic shifts, and competitor actions.
- Researchers isolate the sales impact of promotion either by comparing past sales with past promotion expenditures through statistical modeling or by running controlled field experiments that vary promotional spending across matched geographic markets or digital user groups.

## Socially Responsible Marketing Communications [no-exercise]
- In shaping its promotion mix, a company must be aware of the large body of legal and ethical issues surrounding marketing communications.
- In advertising and sales promotion, companies must avoid false or deceptive advertising, must not make claims that cannot be substantiated by objective evidence, and must not use bait-and-switch advertising that attracts buyers under false pretenses.
- Trade promotions are also regulated so sellers do not offer promotional allowances or services to favored large retailers without making them available on proportionally equal terms to competing resellers.
- In personal selling, salespeople must follow the rules of fair competition: they may not lie to consumers or mislead them about the advantages of buying a product, nor may they disparage competitors' products with false statements or offer bribes to purchasing agents.

## Part 5: Generative AI in Marketing Communications [no-exercise]
- Generative artificial intelligence now drafts advertising copy, product photography, localized translations, and video storyboards in minutes.
- Examining generative AI through the IMC framework reveals why low production cost makes central brand coordination, factual verification, and audience disclosure more important than ever.

## Scaling Creative Production and the Risk of Brand Fragmentation [exercise]
[figure: generative AI content pipeline taking a unified brand brief to generate personalized ad variations while passing through an IMC consistency, factual accuracy, and legal disclosure review gate]
- Generative AI models reduce the marginal cost and time required to produce text copy, visual graphics, voiceovers, and video variations by orders of magnitude.
- This capability allows marketers to create dozens of tailored versions of a campaign message, adapting imagery, tone, and examples to specific demographic segments, local markets, or stages of buyer readiness.
- However, multiplying the number of message variations across channels dramatically increases the core risk that IMC was created to prevent: brand fragmentation and contradictory messaging.
- If different regional teams or automated ad tools prompt generative models independently without a unified creative brief and brand style guardrails, customers encounter disjointed brand voices and conflicting value claims across touchpoints.

## Factual Verification, Disclosure, and Audience Trust in AI Messages [no-exercise]
- Beyond brand consistency, generative AI introduces two critical governance requirements in marketing communications: factual substantiation and synthetic-media disclosure.
- Because language and image models generate outputs probabilistically rather than verifying facts against engineering databases, an unreviewed AI-drafted advertisement can invent product capabilities, misstate warranty terms, or depict product performance that the actual item cannot deliver.
- Under truth-in-advertising laws, the sponsoring company is fully liable for every deceptive claim in its promotions, regardless of whether a human copywriter or a generative model wrote the text.
- Furthermore, audiences increasingly expect, and regulations such as the European Union's AI Act and platform policies increasingly require, clear disclosure when realistic images, audio, or video imitate real people or events, since undisclosed synthetic endorsements can trigger severe source discounting and brand backlash when discovered.

## Discussion: Governing Generative AI in a Global Product Launch Campaign
- Discussion: A global skincare brand plans to use generative AI to produce 200 localized social-video ads across 15 countries, including AI-generated virtual presenters describing the clinical results of a new anti-aging serum. Using the IMC framework, the objective-and-task budget method, source credibility, and AI disclosure regulations, identify the communication and legal risks of this plan and design a review and disclosure protocol before any ad goes live.

## Key Takeaways
- The promotion mix comprises five primary tools (advertising, public relations, personal selling, sales promotion, and direct and digital marketing) that Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) coordinates to deliver a clear, consistent brand message across touchpoints.
- Marketers blend push strategies (using sales forces and trade promotions to push products through channels) and pull strategies (using consumer advertising and promotions to build end-user demand).
- The communication process operates through sender, encoding, message, media, decoding, receiver, response, feedback, and noise, requiring marketers to move target audiences through six buyer-readiness stages (awareness, knowledge, liking, preference, conviction, and purchase).
- Message design combines rational, emotional, or moral content appeals with one-sided or two-sided message structures, strong visual and verbal formats, and credible sources across personal and nonpersonal media channels.
- Among the four promotional budgeting methods (affordable, percentage-of-sales, competitive-parity, and objective-and-task), the objective-and-task method is the most logical because it ties spending directly to specific communication objectives and tasks.
- Generative AI enables rapid, low-cost personalization of marketing messages at scale, which increases the need for strict IMC brand coordination, human factual verification against deceptive claims, and transparent disclosure of synthetic media.
