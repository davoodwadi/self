# Introduction to Marketing - Course Curriculum

**Subtitle**: Creating Value for Customers and Their AI Agents  
**Course Level**: Bachelor's  
**Duration**: 12 Weeks  
**Instructor**: Davood Wadi, PhD  
**Summary**: The fundamental concepts, theories, and practices of modern marketing, from the marketing process and STP to the four Ps, and how artificial intelligence and the AI agents that shop on consumers' behalf are changing each of them.  
**Overview**: This course introduces the fundamental concepts, theories, and practices of modern marketing. Students will explore how organizations create, communicate, and deliver value to target customers while achieving business objectives. Each week also examines how artificial intelligence changes that work: as a tool marketers use to research, design, price, and promote, and as a new actor in the market, the AI agent that searches, compares, and buys on the customer's behalf.

---

## Weekly Breakdown

### Week 1: Introduction to Marketing & The Marketing Process
**Summary**: The definition and evolution of marketing, needs, wants, and demands, and the five-step marketing process. The AI agent as a new kind of customer who acts on a consumer's wants.

- Definition and evolution of marketing.
- Needs, wants, and demands.
- The marketing process and creating customer value.
- Marketing to AI agents: customers who delegate search and purchase to an AI agent.

### Week 2: The Marketing Environment and Ethics
**Summary**: The micro and macro environment, PESTLE, corporate social responsibility, and ethical marketing practices. Artificial intelligence as a technological and legal force, and the ethics of influencing consumers and their AI agents.

- Micro and macro-environmental factors (PESTLE).
- Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR).
- Ethical considerations in marketing practices.
- Artificial intelligence in the marketing environment: disclosure, regulation, and dark patterns directed at AI agents.

### Week 3: Consumer Behavior
**Summary**: The five-step consumer decision process, the cultural, social, and psychological influences on buying, and B2C versus B2B purchasing. The steps of the decision that consumers delegate to AI agents, and the steps they keep.

- The consumer decision-making process.
- Psychological, social, and cultural influences on buying behavior.
- B2C vs B2B purchasing differences.
- Delegating the decision: the steps an AI agent takes over and the steps consumers keep.

### Week 4: Business-to-Business (B2B) Marketing
**Summary**: The characteristics of B2B markets, the buying center and its roles, and relationship marketing with key accounts. AI agents in organizational buying and their place in the buying center.

- Characteristics of B2B markets.
- The organizational buying center.
- Relationship marketing and key account management.
- AI agents in organizational buying: automated sourcing and the buying center.

### Week 5: Marketing Research and Data Analytics
**Summary**: The marketing research process, primary and secondary data, and the role of analytics and customer insights in decisions. Artificial intelligence as a research assistant and as a simulated survey respondent, and the checks its output requires.

- The marketing research process.
- Primary vs. secondary data.
- The role of data analytics and customer insights in decision making.
- AI in marketing research: AI-assisted analysis and simulated survey respondents.

### Week 6: Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning (STP)
**Summary**: Bases for segmenting consumer and business markets, evaluating and selecting targets, and building a value proposition and position. Positioning a brand when an AI assistant builds the shortlist the customer sees.

- Bases for segmenting consumer and business markets.
- Evaluating and selecting target markets.
- Developing a compelling value proposition and positioning strategy.
- Positioning for AI assistants: brand associations and the AI-generated shortlist.

### Week 7: Product and Service Strategies (The First P)
**Summary**: Product classifications, the product life cycle, new product development, branding, packaging, and the characteristics of services. Artificial intelligence in idea generation and concept testing, and services delivered by AI.

- Product classifications and the product life cycle (PLC).
- New product development process.
- Branding, packaging, and the unique characteristics of services.
- AI in new product development: idea generation and concept testing.
- AI-delivered services: chatbots, assistants, and the four service characteristics.

### Week 8: Pricing Strategies (The Second P)
**Summary**: Factors affecting pricing decisions, cost-, value-, and competition-based pricing, and pricing for new products and product mixes. Algorithmic and personalized pricing, and how AI agents compare prices on a consumer's behalf.

- Factors affecting pricing decisions.
- Cost-based, value-based, and competition-based pricing.
- Pricing strategies for new products and product mixes.
- Algorithmic and personalized pricing, and price comparison by AI agents.

### Week 9: Supply Chain and Distribution Channels (The Third P)
**Summary**: The nature and importance of marketing channels, channel behavior and organization, and retailing, wholesaling, and logistics. The AI agent as a new intermediary between the customer and the seller.

- The nature and importance of marketing channels.
- Channel behavior and organization.
- Retailing, wholesaling, and logistics management.
- Agentic commerce: the AI agent as a channel intermediary.

### Week 10: Integrated Marketing Communications (The Fourth P)
**Summary**: The promotion mix, designing an integrated marketing communications strategy, and setting and evaluating the promotional budget. Generative AI in message creation, and its disclosure to the audience.

- The promotion mix: Advertising, PR, Sales Promotion, Personal Selling.
- Designing an integrated marketing communications (IMC) strategy.
- Setting the promotional budget and measuring effectiveness.
- Generative AI in marketing communications: creating and disclosing AI-made messages.

### Week 11: Digital and Social Media Marketing
**Summary**: The shift from traditional to digital marketing, SEO, SEM, content and email marketing, and social media engagement. Search that ends in an AI-generated answer, and sponsored results in the rankings that AI agents read.

- The shift from traditional to digital marketing.
- SEO, SEM, content marketing, and email marketing.
- Leveraging social media platforms for customer engagement.
- From search engines to AI answers: visibility and sponsored results when AI agents search.

### Week 12: Global Marketing and the Future of Marketing
**Summary**: Deciding whether to go global, market entry strategies, and standardized versus adapted marketing mixes. Emerging trends in AI, sustainability, and immersive experiences, and whom AI agents serve when the platform and the customer want different things.

- Deciding whether to go global and market entry strategies.
- Standardized vs. adapted global marketing mixes.
- Emerging trends: AI in marketing, sustainability, and immersive experiences.
- Whom AI agents serve: the customer, the platform, and the future of marketing.
