## Title Slide
- Week 11
- Digital and social media marketing connect brands with customers through interactive owned, paid, and earned channels, while the rise of conversational AI answers and shopping agents reshapes search visibility and sponsored placements.

## Defining Digital and Direct Marketing [no-exercise]
- Direct and digital marketing involves engaging directly with carefully targeted individual consumers and customer communities to both obtain an immediate response and build lasting customer relationships.
- For most companies today, direct and digital marketing are not merely supplementary promotion channels, but a complete model for doing business and interacting with buyers.
- Digital marketing uses electronic devices, the internet, mobile networks, and connected applications to reach buyers wherever they work, shop, and socialize.
- Because digital interactions are logged at the individual session level, marketers can track audience responses in real time and adjust creative messages, targeting, and budgets continuously.

## The Shift from One-Way Broadcast to Two-Way Interactive Engagement [no-exercise]
- Traditional mass marketing relied on one-way broadcast communication: the firm designed a uniform message and projected it via television, radio, or print to a passive mass audience.
- Digital and social media marketing shifts communication to an interactive, two-way dialogue in which consumers actively search for information, ask questions, post public product reviews, and share brand content with peers.
- In this interactive environment, consumers exercise substantial control over which commercial messages they view, skip, block, or amplify.
- Effective digital marketing therefore focuses on customer-initiated attraction and permission-based engagement rather than intrusive interruption alone.

## Part 1: The Digital Marketing Ecosystem [no-exercise]
- A modern digital strategy coordinates three interconnected media categories: owned media, paid media, and earned media.
- Balancing these three media pillars across desktop and mobile touchpoints creates a resilient digital acquisition and retention funnel.

## Owned, Paid, and Earned Digital Media [exercise]
[figure: Venn diagram of the digital marketing ecosystem showing owned media (brand websites and apps), paid media (search and display ads), and earned media (customer reviews, shares, and organic mentions) converging around customer analytics]
- Owned media consist of digital properties that the brand controls directly, including its corporate website, e-commerce storefront, mobile application, blog, and opted-in email subscriber list.
- Paid media consist of external digital placements for which the brand pays to acquire reach and traffic, including paid search advertisements, programmatic display banners, paid social-media promotions, and paid influencer sponsorships.
- Earned media consist of organic exposure generated voluntarily by consumers, journalists, and independent creators through customer reviews, social shares, forum recommendations, and press coverage.
- Owned media build long-term customer equity at low marginal cost, paid media provide immediate scalable traffic for new launches, and earned media supply the third-party credibility that converts skeptical prospects.

## Websites, Landing Pages, and Conversion Optimization [no-exercise]
- For most organizations, the first step in conducting digital marketing is creating a brand website or storefront that serves as the central destination for owned, paid, and earned traffic.
- Whereas a corporate brand website builds general goodwill and answers stakeholder questions, an e-commerce or campaign landing page is engineered to guide visitors toward a specific conversion action, such as completing a purchase, booking a demo, or subscribing to a newsletter.
- To attract repeat visitors and minimize bounce rates, marketers focus on clear navigation, fast page loading speeds, transparent pricing, and frictionless checkout flows.
- Marketers continuously refine digital pages through randomized A/B testing, comparing two versions of a headline, product layout, or call-to-action button on live visitor traffic to measure which design yields a higher conversion rate.

## Mobile Marketing and Omnichannel Integration [no-exercise]
- Mobile marketing features marketing messages, promotions, and other content delivered to on-the-go consumers through their smartphones and wearable devices.
- Because smartphones accompany consumers throughout the day, mobile channels allow brands to deliver time-sensitive, location-aware information and immediate one-tap payment options.
- Shoppers frequently use mobile devices inside physical retail aisles to look up product ratings, compare competitor prices, and redeem digital loyalty rewards.
- Designing mobile-first interfaces ensures that product specifications, reviews, and checkout buttons remain legible and responsive on small screens.

## Part 2: Search, Content, and Email Marketing [no-exercise]
- Search engines, content libraries, and email campaigns form the workhorse channels of digital customer acquisition and retention.
- Comparing organic search optimization with paid search advertising and permission-based email clarifies how firms balance short-term sales with long-term organic equity.

## Search Engine Optimization (SEO) Versus Search Engine Marketing (SEM) [exercise]
[figure: search engine results page contrasting paid Search Engine Marketing (SEM) auction placements at the top with organic Search Engine Optimization (SEO) rankings below]
- Search Engine Optimization (SEO) is the practice of structuring web pages, technical metadata, site speed, and authoritative content so a brand ranks highly in unpaid (organic) search engine results when users query relevant topics.
- Search engines rank organic pages based on relevance to the user's query intent, technical crawlability, and domain authority (signaled by reputable external websites linking to the page).
- Search Engine Marketing (SEM), often called paid search or pay-per-click (PPC) advertising, involves bidding on specific keywords in real-time ad auctions so a sponsored text or product listing appears above or beside organic search results.
- SEM delivers immediate, predictable visibility as soon as a campaign budget is turned on (and stops the moment spending stops), whereas SEO requires months of sustained content investment but compounds into durable, high-trust traffic without a per-click ad fee.

## Content Marketing Strategy [no-exercise]
- Content marketing is the strategic creation and distribution of valuable, relevant, and consistent content (such as educational guides, comparison articles, how-to videos, webinars, and interactive calculators) to attract and retain a clearly defined audience.
- Rather than pitching a product directly in every message, content marketing solves practical problems that target buyers research during the early need-recognition and information-search stages of their journey.
- A home-improvement retailer that publishes step-by-step tile-installation videos and mortar calculators builds authority and trust with homeowners, who then purchase the required tools and materials from that retailer.
- High-quality educational content also fuels both organic SEO rankings and social media sharing.

## Permission-Based Email Marketing and Lifecycle Automation [exercise]
- Email marketing remains one of the highest-return digital channels because it reaches an opted-in audience directly without paying an intermediary platform for each impression.
- Effective email marketing relies on segmentation and behavioral lifecycle automation rather than blasting identical promotional flyers to an entire mailing list.
- Automated lifecycle triggers send timely, relevant messages based on a customer's specific actions: a welcome sequence after account creation, a helpful reminder after an abandoned shopping cart, a care guide after product delivery, or a replenishment prompt when a consumable item is due to run out.
- Conversely, sending unsolicited commercial email (spam) violates anti-spam laws (such as CAN-SPAM in the United States and CASL in Canada), triggers spam filters, and damages brand reputation.

## Discussion: Allocating a Startup Budget Across Paid Search, SEO Content, and Email
- Discussion: A direct-to-consumer ergonomic desk manufacturer has a 15,000-dollar monthly digital marketing budget and needs both immediate first-quarter sales to pay suppliers and lower customer acquisition costs over the next two years. Using SEO, SEM (pay-per-click), content marketing, and permission-based email automation, design a phased allocation of the budget and explain how each channel supports the others across the customer journey.

## Part 3: Leveraging Social Media for Customer Engagement [no-exercise]
- Social media are independent and commercial online communities where people congregate to socialize and share messages, opinions, pictures, videos, and other content.
- Marketers participate in social networks to build brand communities, listen to customer sentiment, and partner with credible creators.

## Social Media Platforms, Demographics, and Use Cases [exercise]
- Rather than treating social media as a single uniform channel, marketers match their content formats and objectives to the distinct demographics and usage norms of each platform.
- Visual discovery and lifestyle platforms (such as Instagram and Pinterest) excel at showcasing aesthetics, fashion, food, and home design during early inspiration and consideration.
- Short-form video platforms (such as TikTok and YouTube Shorts) rely on algorithmic interest graphs rather than follower graphs alone, enabling rapid organic reach and product demonstrations among younger and mainstream audiences.
- Professional networks (such as LinkedIn) are essential for B2B thought leadership, executive networking, and account-based marketing, while discussion forums (such as Reddit) host detailed peer product evaluations.

## Building Customer Engagement and Social Listening [no-exercise]
- Because social media are interactive, using them solely to broadcast one-way sales announcements generates low engagement.
- Successful brands foster genuine customer engagement by creating shareable stories, responding promptly and helpfully to customer service inquiries in public threads, and encouraging user-generated content (UGC) from real buyers.
- Social listening is the systematic monitoring of digital conversations across social networks, forums, and review sites to understand what customers are saying about a brand, its competitors, and its industry.
- Unfiltered social listening helps marketers detect emerging product defects or service crises early, uncover unmet customer use cases, and adopt the natural vocabulary buyers use to describe their problems.

## Influencer Marketing and Creator Partnerships [no-exercise]
- Influencer marketing involves partnering with individuals who have built a dedicated social following and are viewed as knowledgeable or relatable opinion leaders within a specific niche.
- Macro-influencers and celebrities offer massive broad reach for awareness campaigns, whereas micro-influencers (with smaller, highly focused audiences) frequently generate higher engagement rates and stronger purchase conversion because followers view their recommendations as authentic peer advice.
- In accordance with the match-up hypothesis, influencer partnerships succeed only when the creator's expertise and values align naturally with the product category.
- Consumer protection agencies require both brands and creators to disclose material connections clearly (for example, using prominent `#Ad` or `Paid Partnership` labels) so audiences are not deceived into mistaking paid promotions for independent reviews.

## Part 4: Measuring Digital ROI and Protecting Consumer Privacy [no-exercise]
- Although digital channels generate abundant metrics, translating clicks and impressions into true incremental profit requires disciplined attribution and privacy governance.
- Marketers must separate vanity metrics from actionable business outcomes while respecting data protection laws.

## Digital Metrics and Conversion Attribution [exercise]
- Digital platforms report dozens of performance indicators across the funnel: impressions (total views), click-through rate (clicks divided by impressions), cost per click (CPC), conversion rate (purchases divided by clicks), customer acquisition cost (CAC), and return on ad spend (ROAS).
- A common pitfall in social and digital reporting is optimizing for vanity metrics, such as raw follower counts, video views, or post likes, that look impressive on a slide but do not correlate with actual sales or retention.
- Because a single customer may see a social video, read a blog post, and click a paid brand search link before buying, marketers use attribution modeling (such as first-touch, last-touch, or multi-touch attribution) to assign credit across touchpoints.
- To measure true causal incrementality rather than mere correlation, analytics teams run randomized holdout experiments that compare conversion rates with and without the digital campaign.

## Digital Privacy Regulations and the Shift to First-Party Data [no-exercise]
- Digital targeting historically relied heavily on third-party cookies and cross-app tracking identifiers that followed users silently across unrelated websites.
- Growing consumer concern over surveillance and strict privacy laws, including the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and state privacy laws in North America, have restricted non-consensual third-party tracking.
- Major web browsers and mobile operating systems have likewise deprecated third-party cookies and mandated explicit opt-in tracking prompts.
- Forward-looking marketers are replacing opaque third-party tracking with transparent first-party data strategies built on direct customer accounts, loyalty programs, and contextual advertising that matches ads to page content rather than personal surveillance profiles.

## Ethical Boundaries in Behavioral Targeting and Personalization [no-exercise]
- Even when data collection complies with statutory law, aggressive behavioral profiling can cross ethical boundaries and alienate customers.
- Retargeting ads that follow a shopper across dozens of websites after a single product view often trigger feelings of surveillance and reactance rather than goodwill.
- Targeting vulnerable consumers based on inferred financial distress, health anxieties, or compulsive habits violates marketing ethics and damages long-term brand trust.
- Giving consumers clear preference centers to view, edit, or delete their stored profile data builds durable trust alongside personalized convenience.

## Part 5: From Search Engines to AI Answers and Agent Rankings [no-exercise]
- Search behavior is undergoing a structural shift as consumers ask conversational AI assistants for synthesized answers and delegate shopping searches to autonomous AI agents.
- Empirical research on how AI agents process ranked lists and sponsorship disclosures shows how both organic search and paid search are changing.

## From Ten Blue Links to Conversational AI Answers [no-exercise]
[figure: comparison of traditional keyword search returning ten web links for the human user to click against conversational AI search reading sources and returning a synthesized answer and product shortlist]
- For two decades, digital search followed a consistent pattern: a consumer typed a short keyword phrase into a search engine, received a page of links, and clicked through to visit individual brand websites.
- Increasingly, consumers ask conversational AI assistants complete questions and receive a single synthesized answer and product recommendation list directly inside the chat interface, often without clicking out to external websites (zero-click search).
- In this environment, brand visibility depends on whether the AI system retrieves and cites the brand's content when constructing its answer, a discipline sometimes called generative engine optimization (GEO).
- Clear, factual, well-structured documentation, authoritative third-party reviews, and consistent schema markup help a brand earn inclusion in both traditional organic search results and AI-generated answers.

## List Position Effects When AI Agents Search [exercise]
- When an AI shopping agent searches an online marketplace or search engine on a consumer's behalf, the agent reads ranked product lists rather than browsing a physical store shelf.
- One might assume that a software agent evaluates every item on a page equally regardless of rank order, but empirical studies demonstrate the opposite (Wadi & Ma, 2026a).
- Controlled experiments show that AI shopping agents exhibit strong position effects: agents favor options simply because of where they appear in a ranked list, even when objective product attributes and prices are held constant (Wadi & Ma, 2026a).
- Securing top rank placement in marketplace search feeds and structured data indices therefore remains critical when the searcher is an AI agent rather than a human shopper (Wadi & Ma, 2026a).

## Sponsored Labels and Paid Placements in Agent-Mediated Search [exercise]
[figure: experimental comparison showing AI shopping agents favoring top-ranked list positions and selecting a paid listing less often when labeled "Sponsored" than when labeled "Promoted"]
- Paid search placements and retail media ads now reach AI shopping agents alongside human consumers, raising the question of how agents react to disclosure labels.
- In human search, regulators require paid listings to carry clear labels so shoppers can distinguish paid ads from organic rankings.
- When AI agents evaluate ranked product lists, the exact wording of the disclosure label significantly alters agent choice: AI agents chose a paid listing less often when it was labeled "Sponsored" than when the identical paid listing was labeled "Promoted" (Wadi & Ma, 2026c).
- Digital marketers bidding on retail media placements and policy makers standardizing ad disclosures must therefore account for how AI agents interpret sponsorship labels (Wadi & Ma, 2026c).

## Discussion: Adapting Search and Paid Media Strategy for AI Shopping Agents
- Discussion: An online travel and outdoor-gear retailer spends 60 percent of its digital budget bidding on paid search placements labeled "Sponsored" or "Promoted" and 40 percent on SEO blog articles written for human keyword searches. Drawing on conversational AI answers, list position effects in AI agents (Wadi & Ma, 2026a), and the differential impact of "Sponsored" versus "Promoted" labels on AI agent choice (Wadi & Ma, 2026c), how should the retailer audit and adjust its search content and paid placement strategy as more customers shop through AI assistants?

## Key Takeaways
- Digital and social media marketing shift customer communication from one-way mass broadcasting to interactive, measurable, two-way engagement across desktop and mobile devices.
- A balanced digital ecosystem integrates owned media (websites, apps, and email lists), paid media (search and display ads), and earned media (organic reviews, shares, and press mentions).
- Search Engine Optimization (SEO) builds long-term organic visibility through relevant content and site authority, whereas Search Engine Marketing (SEM) buys immediate top-of-page visibility through keyword auctions, complemented by educational content marketing and lifecycle email automation.
- Social media strategy matches content to platform-specific demographics and norms, combining community engagement, social listening, and transparently disclosed influencer partnerships.
- Digital performance evaluation prioritizes incremental conversion attribution and return on ad spend over vanity metrics, while privacy regulations and browser changes shift targeting toward consented first-party data.
- As search moves from keyword links to conversational AI answers and delegated AI shopping agents, visibility depends on structured factual content, list rank position because AI agents exhibit position effects (Wadi & Ma, 2026a), and sponsorship label wording because AI agents penalize listings labeled "Sponsored" more than those labeled "Promoted" (Wadi & Ma, 2026c).
