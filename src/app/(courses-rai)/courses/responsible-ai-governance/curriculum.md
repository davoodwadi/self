# Responsible AI & Algorithmic Governance - Course Curriculum

**Subtitle**: Ethics, Auditing, and Regulation in Human and Agent-Mediated Markets  
**Course Level**: Master's  
**Program**: Marketing & Business Analytics  
**Duration**: 12 Weeks  
**Instructor**: Davood Wadi, PhD  
**Summary**: Examines how organizations audit, govern, and deploy artificial intelligence responsibly in market settings. Topics include algorithmic fairness, cultural bias, behavioral auditing, explainability, data privacy, dark patterns, fiduciary duty in AI agents, and global regulation.  
**Overview**: This course examines how organizations design, audit, and govern artificial intelligence systems that interact with consumers and shape market outcomes. Students analyze ethical frameworks, fairness trade-offs, privacy rules, and regulatory regimes, and pair policy analysis with empirical behavioral audits of large language models and autonomous shopping agents. Across the curriculum, artificial intelligence is studied both as an organizational tool that requires oversight and as a market actor whose biases, disclosures, and conflicts of duty must be measured and governed.

---

## Weekly Breakdown

### Week 1: Foundations of Responsible AI in Markets
**Summary**: Ethical frameworks for evaluating technology in business, stakeholder responsibility, and why consumer-facing and agent-mediated markets create distinct governance risks.

- Consequentialist, deontological, and virtue ethics applied to automated business decisions.
- Stakeholder responsibility and accountability across model developers, deploying firms, and platforms.
- The governance gap between technical benchmark performance and real-world market behavior.
- Human-in-the-loop, human-on-the-loop, and autonomous decision regimes in marketing and operations.
- Delegated consumption and why AI shopping agents introduce new questions of agency and liability.

### Week 2: Algorithmic Bias, Fairness, and Discrimination
**Summary**: Sources of bias across data collection, model training, and deployment, formal definitions of group and individual fairness, and the impossibility of satisfying all fairness criteria simultaneously.

- Historical, representation, measurement, and aggregation bias in customer data.
- Group fairness metrics, including demographic parity, equalized odds, and predictive parity.
- Individual fairness, counterfactual fairness, and calibration across protected groups.
- The fairness impossibility theorem and managerial trade-offs in credit scoring, ad delivery, and hiring.
- Auditing automated targeting and personalization pipelines for disparate impact.

### Week 3: Cultural Bias and Representation in Language Models
**Summary**: Demographic stereotyping, cultural alignment, and consumer ethnocentrism in large language models, and how country-of-origin biases distort product recommendations across global markets.

- Training corpus composition and the over-representation of dominant cultural norms in foundation models.
- Stereotyping, erasure, and sentiment disparities across demographic groups in generated text and imagery.
- Consumer ethnocentrism and in-group favoritism in large language models compared against human baselines.
- Country-of-origin bias in AI product recommendations and its effect on domestic and foreign brands.
- Mitigating cultural bias through prompt design, fine-tuning, and distributional evaluation.

### Week 4: Behavioral Auditing and Statistical Evaluation of AI Systems
**Summary**: Moving beyond static single-sample benchmarks to statistically grounded behavioral audits using Monte-Carlo sampling, token-level probability distributions, and controlled factorial experiments.

- Why single-prompt evaluations fail to capture stochastic model behavior and latent attitudes.
- Monte-Carlo sampling frameworks for estimating model preferences with statistical guarantees.
- Exact Likert-scale probability distributions from token-level probabilities and distributional analysis of variance.
- Designing factorial choice and rating experiments to isolate prompt sensitivity, framing, and attribute weights.
- Pre-deployment versus post-deployment behavioral auditing workflows for enterprise AI systems.

### Week 5: Transparency, Explainability, and Reasoning Traces
**Summary**: Interpretable models versus post-hoc explanation methods, the faithfulness of chain-of-thought reasoning traces in language models, and what explanations owe to managers, regulators, and consumers.

- Intrinsic interpretability versus black-box complexity in predictive marketing models.
- Post-hoc feature attribution methods, including permutation importance, SHAP, and LIME.
- Counterfactual explanations and actionable recourse for consumers affected by automated decisions.
- Reasoning traces in large language models as verbal protocols and where stated reasoning diverges from actual choice.
- Designing disclosures and model documentation for technical teams, executives, and end users.

### Week 6: Privacy, Consent, and Data Governance
**Summary**: Data provenance, contextual integrity, privacy-preserving analytics, and research ethics governance for studies involving human participants and synthetic AI respondents.

- First-party, observed, inferred, and scraped data provenance in training and personalization.
- Contextual integrity, meaningful informed consent, and the limits of notice-and-choice in digital ecosystems.
- Technical privacy safeguards, including anonymization risks, differential privacy, and machine unlearning.
- Research Ethics Board standards for human-subjects research, deception, and data protection.
- Ethical boundaries of using synthetic AI respondents to supplement or replace human consumer panels.

### Week 7: Manipulation, Dark Patterns, and Consumer Autonomy
**Summary**: Digital choice architecture, algorithmic hyper-persuasion, sycophancy, and deceptive interface patterns directed at human consumers and at the AI agents that shop on their behalf.

- Nudges versus sludges and the taxonomy of dark patterns in digital storefronts.
- Generative personalization, emotional targeting, and anthropomorphic design in conversational interfaces.
- Model sycophancy and the reinforcement of user beliefs in AI assistants.
- Adversarial manipulation of AI shopping agents through hidden prompt injections and strategic attribute framing.
- Measuring consumer autonomy, cognitive vulnerability, and welfare losses in manipulative environments.

### Week 8: Fiduciary Duty and Conflicts of Interest in Agentic Commerce
**Summary**: Principal-agent relationships when advertising-funded platforms deploy conversational shopping assistants, sponsorship bias, position effects, and why human-centric disclosures fail in agent-mediated markets.

- The AI shopping assistant as a surrogate consumer and the legal and ethical concept of fiduciary duty.
- Conflicts of duty when a platform-deployed agent serves both the shopper and the advertising platform.
- How role assignment in system prompts alters an agent's penalty for sponsored and promoted listings.
- Non-monotonic position bias and information acquisition patterns when AI agents inspect ranked search results.
- Rethinking sponsorship disclosure mandates and structural separation for agent-mediated marketplaces.

### Week 9: Algorithmic Pricing, Collusion, and Fair Competition
**Summary**: Dynamic and personalized pricing, algorithmic price discrimination, tacit coordination among pricing algorithms, and competition policy in automated digital markets.

- Dynamic pricing, surge pricing, and first-degree price discrimination using granular consumer profiles.
- Consumer fairness perceptions and trust erosion under personalized algorithmic pricing.
- Tacit algorithmic collusion when reinforcement-learning pricing agents interact repeatedly in shared markets.
- Self-preferencing, gatekeeper power, and algorithmic ranking on multi-sided digital platforms.
- Antitrust enforcement, algorithmic price-fixing liability, and auditing market algorithms for anti-competitive behavior.

### Week 10: Accessibility, Inclusion, and Human-Centered AI Design
**Summary**: Universal design principles, assistive AI systems for learners and consumers with disabilities, digital divides, and keeping human judgment effective in AI-supported workflows.

- Disability inclusion, universal design, and accessibility standards in digital products and services.
- Designing AI-powered learning and decision support systems for users with diverse cognitive and physical needs.
- Language, literacy, and socioeconomic barriers to equitable participation in AI-mediated services.
- Automation bias, deskilling, and alert fatigue in human-on-the-loop organizational workflows.
- Participatory design and co-creation with marginalized and underrepresented stakeholder groups.

### Week 11: Global AI Regulation, Standards, and Compliance
**Summary**: Comparative analysis of risk-based AI legislation, consumer protection enforcement, privacy laws, and international management standards governing commercial AI deployment.

- The European Union AI Act and its risk tiers, prohibited practices, and obligations for general-purpose AI models.
- Canadian privacy and artificial intelligence governance frameworks, including PIPEDA, Quebec Law 25, and federal AI policy.
- United States regulatory enforcement through the Federal Trade Commission on unfair or deceptive AI practices.
- Voluntary governance frameworks and technical standards, including the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and ISO/IEC 42001.
- Translating statutory requirements into product requirements, conformity assessments, and audit trails.

### Week 12: Organizational AI Governance, Red-Teaming, and Sustainability
**Summary**: Building internal AI ethics and review boards, red-teaming protocols, incident response, environmental and computational resource rationality, and continuous post-deployment monitoring.

- Structuring internal AI governance committees, review gates, and cross-functional accountability (RACI).
- Model cards, datasheets for datasets, and system-level impact assessments before launch.
- Adversarial red-teaming, stress-testing, and incident response playbooks for generative and agentic deployments.
- Resource rationality, computational efficiency, and the environmental footprint of training and inference.
- Designing a comprehensive organizational governance charter for human and agent-mediated markets.
