# Digital Marketing & E-Commerce - Course Curriculum

**Subtitle**: Platforms, Algorithms, and Agent-Mediated Commerce  
**Course Level**: Undergraduate  
**Program**: Marketing & Information Systems  
**Duration**: 12 Weeks  
**Instructor**: Davood Wadi, PhD  
**Summary**: Examines how digital channels, e-commerce platforms, and marketplace algorithms acquire, convert, and retain customers. Topics include storefront architecture, search and recommender systems, programmatic advertising, social commerce, CRM and lifecycle automation, online reviews, dynamic pricing, omnichannel fulfillment, multi-touch attribution, and autonomous AI shopping agents.  
**Overview**: This course sits at the intersection of marketing strategy and information systems. Students examine how digital storefronts and multi-sided marketplaces are architected, how algorithmic channels such as search engines, recommender systems, programmatic ad exchanges, and social feeds allocate consumer attention, and how firms manage conversion, online reputation, pricing, fulfillment, and attribution. Across every topic, the course tracks the shift from human-browsed web storefronts to agent-mediated commerce, where AI assistants search, evaluate rankings, read reviews, and transact on the customer's behalf.

---

## Weekly Breakdown

### Week 1: Digital Business Models and E-Commerce Architecture
**Summary**: Direct-to-consumer, marketplace, and retail media business models alongside the modern e-commerce technology stack, from monolithic platforms to headless APIs and agent-accessible catalogs.

- Digital commerce models across B2C, B2B, D2C, two-sided marketplaces, and subscription commerce.
- Multi-sided platform economics, including network effects, take rates, and retail media networks.
- The e-commerce information systems stack, comparing monolithic suites with headless and composable commerce, APIs, and payment gateways.
- Mapping the digital customer funnel from acquisition to conversion, fulfillment, and retention.
- From web browsers to autonomous agents, and how AI shopping assistants interact with digital storefronts and product feeds.

### Week 2: Storefront Design, Information Architecture, and Conversion
**Summary**: User experience, site taxonomy, product page design, and conversion rate optimization for human shoppers, contrasted with structured data and information acquisition costs for AI agents.

- Storefront taxonomy, search and filter navigation, and product detail page design.
- Conversion rate optimization, funnel diagnostics, checkout friction, and cart abandonment recovery.
- Digital choice architecture, including defaults, scarcity cues, urgency timers, and the boundary with deceptive dark patterns.
- Machine-readable storefronts built on HTML DOM structure, Schema.org product markup, and API feeds.
- Information acquisition costs in agentic shopping, and how storefront layout and attribute placement shape what AI agents inspect and buy.

### Week 3: Search Engine Marketing, Organic Search, and Paid Search
**Summary**: Search engine crawling, indexing, and ranking, paid search auction mechanics and bidding strategies, incrementality, and visibility in conversational AI answer engines.

- How search engines crawl, index, interpret query intent, and rank pages.
- On-page, technical, and off-page search engine optimization (SEO).
- Paid search (SEM) mechanics, including generalized second-price auctions, Ad Rank, Quality Score, and automated bidding.
- Search cannibalization and measuring when paid search substitutes for or adds to organic clicks.
- From search engines to answer engines, including generative engine optimization (GEO) and entering the shortlist when AI assistants synthesize answers.

### Week 4: Recommender Systems, Search Rankings, and Retail Media
**Summary**: How marketplace recommendation and ranking algorithms order products, why rank position drives human clicks, and how position bias and sponsored slots affect AI shopping agents.

- E-commerce discovery algorithms, including collaborative filtering, content-based filtering, and two-stage retrieval and ranking pipelines.
- Why rank position shapes sales through visual attention, search costs, and steep click decay down the results list.
- Retail media and sponsored search, balancing marketplace advertising revenue against organic relevance.
- Position bias in agent-mediated shopping, comparing how AI agents scan ranked lists with human shoppers.
- Sponsored listings and conflicts of duty when platform-deployed AI assistants recommend products.

### Week 5: Programmatic Advertising and AdTech Infrastructure
**Summary**: The information systems behind digital display and video advertising, including real-time bidding exchanges, audience targeting, ad verification, and privacy-preserving targeting.

- The programmatic AdTech stack, connecting demand-side platforms (DSPs), supply-side platforms (SSPs), and real-time bidding (RTB) auctions.
- Audience targeting with first-, second-, and third-party data, behavioral retargeting, and lookalike modeling.
- Ad verification and market failures, including viewability standards, invalid traffic, ad fraud, and brand safety controls.
- The post-cookie transition through third-party cookie deprecation, data clean rooms, and server-side conversion APIs.
- Generative AI in advertising operations for dynamic creative optimization and automated media buying.

### Week 6: Social Media Marketing, Creator Economies, and Social Commerce
**Summary**: Algorithmic feed curation, organic and paid social strategy, influencer contracts and authenticity, livestream and in-app checkout, and virtual influencers.

- Social platform mechanics, contrasting social graphs with interest graphs and algorithmic feed ranking.
- Paid social advertising, creative testing, and full-funnel social campaign design.
- The creator economy and influencer marketing, including partner selection, compensation structures, and sponsorship disclosure rules.
- Social commerce and livestream shopping that combine discovery, community proof, and checkout inside a single application.
- Virtual influencers and AI-generated brand content, and their effects on consumer trust, authenticity, and disclosure.

### Week 7: Content Strategy, CRM Systems, and Lifecycle Automation
**Summary**: Owned media and content marketing paired with customer relationship management (CRM) systems, customer data platforms (CDPs), and automated lifecycle email and retention flows.

- Content marketing strategy that maps educational, comparison, and transactional content across the buying journey.
- Customer data infrastructure, including CRM systems, customer data platforms (CDPs), identity resolution, and unified customer profiles.
- Event-driven lifecycle automation for welcome series, browse and cart abandonment triggers, replenishment, and win-back flows.
- Email and push notification deliverability, list hygiene, segmentation, and frequency management.
- Hyper-personalization at scale, using language models to tailor lifecycle messages without triggering customer reactance.

### Week 8: Online Reviews, Reputation Management, and User-Generated Content
**Summary**: The economics of electronic word-of-mouth, designing review solicitation messages and incentives without crowding out intrinsic motives or distorting review quality, and AI review summarization.

- Electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) and how review volume, valence, variance, and recency drive e-commerce conversion.
- Soliciting online reviews and the interplay between intrinsic altruism and extrinsic financial incentives in solicitation messaging.
- Incentive design pitfalls and how performance-contingent incentives alter review effort, sentiment, and credibility.
- Platform reputation systems, including verified-purchase badges, fake and synthetic review detection, and seller responses.
- AI-generated review summaries, and how human shoppers and AI shopping agents weigh aggregated ratings against review text.

### Week 9: E-Commerce Pricing, Promotions, and Dynamic Revenue Management
**Summary**: Online pricing strategies, reference price framing, targeted digital promotions, dynamic pricing algorithms, and how AI agents evaluate price-quality trade-offs and discounts.

- Digital pricing architectures, including dynamic pricing, personalized pricing, subscriptions, freemium tiers, and product bundling.
- Promotional framing in digital storefronts using strike-through reference prices, just-below pricing, flash sales, and minimum-spend thresholds.
- Targeted couponing, price discrimination, and managing margin erosion and deal-waiting behavior.
- Algorithmic pricing competition, automated repricers, price-matching bots, and tacit coordination risks on marketplaces.
- How AI shopping agents respond to price anchors, promotional badges, and attribute trade-offs.

### Week 10: Omnichannel Retailing, Fulfillment, and Reverse Logistics
**Summary**: Connecting digital orders to physical supply chains, including unified inventory systems, last-mile delivery economics, shipping fee design, and the e-commerce returns problem.

- Omnichannel retailing and information systems integration, including real-time inventory visibility, buy-online-pick-up-in-store (BOPIS), and ship-from-store.
- Showrooming and webrooming, harmonizing pricing, assortment, and sales incentives across physical and digital channels.
- Fulfillment and last-mile economics, covering warehouse automation, delivery speed promises, and free-shipping threshold design.
- Reverse logistics and e-commerce returns, including return policy leniency, bracketing, restocking costs, and recommerce channels.
- Post-purchase digital experience through proactive order tracking and AI-assisted customer service triage.

### Week 11: Web Analytics, Multi-Touch Attribution, and Unit Economics
**Summary**: Instrumentation of web and app events, e-commerce unit economics, and comparing heuristic, data-driven, and experimental attribution models.

- Digital telemetry and instrumentation using event tracking schemas, pixels, server-side tagging, and cohort retention tables.
- E-commerce unit economics, including customer acquisition cost (CAC), customer lifetime value (CLV), contribution margin, and payback period.
- Return on ad spend (ROAS) versus incremental margin, and why high ROAS can mask zero incremental lift.
- Multi-touch attribution (MTA), comparing rule-based heuristics with Shapley value and Markov models and with geo-lift and holdout experiments.
- Measuring dark-funnel and agent-mediated traffic when product discovery occurs inside conversational AI interfaces.

### Week 12: Privacy, Platform Governance, and the Future of Agentic Commerce
**Summary**: Privacy regulation and consent architectures, platform antitrust and gatekeeper rules, and designing business-to-agent (B2A) strategies for autonomous commerce ecosystems.

- Privacy regulation in digital marketing, including GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, PIPEDA, Quebec Law 25, and consent management platforms (CMPs).
- Platform governance and competition policy, addressing self-preferencing, marketplace data use, and gatekeeper obligations.
- Business-to-agent (B2A) commerce, including agent payment protocols, authenticated delegation, and machine-to-machine negotiation.
- Auditing digital storefronts and marketing algorithms for fairness, manipulation, and principal-agent alignment.
- Building resilient digital marketing and e-commerce systems across human and agent-mediated markets.
