## Title Slide
- Week 04
- Every purchase is an attempt to move from a current state to a better one.

## What Is Consumer Motivation? [no-exercise]
- Motivation is the process that drives people to act when they feel a gap between their current state and a desired state.
- A need creates tension. The consumer takes action to reduce that tension.
- The same product can serve different motives for different people.
- A gym membership may help one person seek health, another seek belonging, and another seek status.
- Marketers study motives so they can connect an offer with a goal that matters to the consumer.

## Drive Theory: Tension and Reduction [exercise]
- Drive theory says that an unmet need creates an uncomfortable internal state called a drive.
- The drive pushes the consumer toward an action that reduces the discomfort.
- Hunger creates a drive to find food. Thirst creates a drive to find a drink.
- After the need is satisfied, tension falls and the consumer returns to a more balanced state.
- Marketing can remind people of a need or show how a product may reduce the resulting drive.

## Expectancy Theory: Effort, Performance, and Outcome [exercise]
- Expectancy theory says that motivation depends on what people believe will happen after they act.
- Expectancy is the belief that effort will lead to good performance.
- Instrumentality is the belief that good performance will lead to a desired outcome.
- Valence is the value the consumer places on that outcome.
- A consumer is more motivated when the goal seems possible, the product seems useful, and the result feels valuable.

## Needs, Wants, and Demand [exercise]
- A need is a basic biological or psychological requirement, such as hunger, safety, or belonging.
- A want is the specific product or service chosen to satisfy a need.
- Culture, personality, and past experience shape wants.
- Demand is a want supported by both the ability and willingness to pay.
- A marketer can shape a want. A marketer cannot create the basic human need underneath it.

## Maslow's Hierarchy in Consumer Markets [exercise]
- Maslow proposed that human needs can be arranged from basic needs to higher-order needs.
- Physiological needs include food, water, and sleep.
- Safety needs include protection, stability, and health.
- Belonging and esteem needs include friendship, acceptance, achievement, and status.
- Self-actualization involves growth and becoming the person one wants to be.
- Consumers can pursue several levels at the same time. The hierarchy is a guide, not a strict shopping sequence.

## Motivational Conflicts: Three Difficult Choices [exercise]
- Approach-approach conflict occurs when a consumer wants two attractive options but can choose only one.
- Approach-avoidance conflict occurs when the same option has both an attractive benefit and an unpleasant cost.
- Avoidance-avoidance conflict occurs when every available option has an unwanted consequence.
- A buyer may want a new phone and a weekend trip, but the budget allows only one.
- Strong brands reduce conflict by making benefits clear and costs easier to understand.

## Consumer Involvement: How Much Does It Matter?
- Involvement is the personal importance a consumer assigns to a product, message, or purchase situation.
- Product involvement is high when the product affects identity, risk, or daily life.
- Message involvement is high when the consumer pays close attention to the information in an advertisement.
- Purchase situation involvement changes with time pressure, social setting, and perceived risk.
- High involvement leads to more effort and careful comparison. Low involvement often leads to habit and simple cues.

## Values Guide Consumer Choices [exercise]
- Values are enduring beliefs about what is important or desirable.
- Functional values focus on performance, convenience, and reliability.
- Social values focus on belonging, recognition, and how others see the consumer.
- Experiential values focus on pleasure, excitement, comfort, or meaning.
- Consumers often choose between products by asking which one fits their values, not only which one has the most features.

## Discussion: Find the Motive
- Discussion: Choose a recent purchase. What need or value did it serve? Which theory best explains your choice: drive reduction, expectancy, hierarchy of needs, or motivational conflict?
