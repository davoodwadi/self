# Consumer Behavior - Course Curriculum

**Course Level**: Bachelor's (Marketing Major)  
**Duration**: 12 Weeks  
**Instructor**: Davood Wadi, PhD  
**Overview**: This course explores why people buy, use, and stop using goods and services. Students examine the internal psychological processes and external social influences that shape consumer choices. The curriculum bridges core behavioral theories with everyday marketing decisions.

---

## Weekly Breakdown

### Week 1: Introduction to Consumer Behavior
- What consumer behavior is and why it matters for marketers.
- The consumer journey: prepurchase, purchase, and postpurchase stages.
- Consumers as individuals versus organizational buyers.
- Marketing ethics, consumer well-being, and dark patterns in sales.

### Week 2: Perception and Sensory Marketing
- The perception process: exposure, attention, and interpretation.
- Sensory marketing: how vision, sound, smell, touch, and taste guide buying.
- Sensory thresholds and Weber's Law in brand package changes.
- Perceptual defense, selective attention, and perceptual vigilance.

### Week 3: Learning and Memory
- Behavioral learning theories: classical and operant conditioning.
- Cognitive learning and observational learning in marketing campaigns.
- The memory system: sensory memory, short-term memory, and long-term memory.
- Brand equity, associative networks, and brand retrieval cues.

### Week 4: Motivation, Needs, and Values
- Motivation as a psychological drive: drive theory and expectancy theory.
- Distinguishing needs from wants; Maslow's hierarchy of needs in consumer markets.
- Motivational conflicts: approach-approach, approach-avoidance, and avoidance-avoidance.
- Consumer involvement: product, message, and purchase situation involvement.

### Week 5: Personality, Self-Concept, and Lifestyles
- The self-concept: actual self versus ideal self.
- Extended self and the products we use to define our identity.
- Personality traits and brand personality dimensions.
- Psychographics and lifestyles: measuring activities, interests, and opinions (AIOs).

### Week 6: Attitudes and Persuasion
- The nature and functions of consumer attitudes.
- The ABC model of attitudes: affect, behavior, and cognition.
- Attitude formation hierarchies: standard learning, low-involvement, and experiential.
- Persuasion and changing attitudes: source credibility, source attractiveness, and message framing.

### Week 7: Consumer Decision-Making Process
- The five-stage rational decision process: problem recognition to postpurchase evaluation.
- Levels of consumer decision making: routine response, limited problem solving, and extended problem solving.
- Information search strategies: internal search versus external search.
- Evaluating options: evoked set, consideration set, and decision rules.

### Week 8: Behavioral Economics, Heuristics, and Biases
- System 1 and System 2 thinking in consumer choices.
- Mental shortcuts (heuristics): availability, representativeness, and price-quality inferences.
- Cognitive biases: anchoring, loss aversion, status quo bias, and framing effects.
- Choice architecture, defaults, and nudge theory in consumer environments.

### Week 9: Post-Purchase Behavior, Satisfaction, and Loyalty
- The gap between expectation and reality: customer satisfaction models.
- Cognitive dissonance and buyer's regret.
- Post-purchase dissonance reduction strategies for marketers.
- Product disposal, secondary markets, and the transition to customer loyalty.

### Week 10: Social Influences, Reference Groups, and Word of Mouth
- Reference groups: membership, aspirational, and dissociative groups.
- Social power: referent, legitimate, expert, reward, and coercive power.
- Word-of-mouth (WOM) communication and opinion leadership.
- Influencer marketing and online communities as social proof.

### Week 11: Culture, Subcultures, and Social Class
- Culture as the lens through which consumers view products.
- Cultural values, rituals, myths, and sacred versus profane consumption.
- Subcultures based on age, generation, geography, and ethnicity.
- Social class, status symbols, conspicuous consumption, and income inequality.

### Week 12: Digital Consumption and Future Trends
- The digital consumer: mobile shopping, social commerce, and algorithm-driven discovery.
- AI, personalized recommendations, and virtual assistants in buying decisions.
- Ethical issues in data collection, surveillance, and algorithmic pricing.
- Sustainable consumption and the rise of the conscious consumer.
