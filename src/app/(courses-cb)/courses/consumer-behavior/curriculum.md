# Consumer Behavior - Course Curriculum

**Course Level**: Bachelor's
**Duration**: 12 Weeks
**Overview**: This course examines how individuals and groups select, purchase, use, and dispose of products, services, and experiences. Students will connect psychological, social, and cultural theory to concrete marketing decisions, and learn to read consumer research critically.

## Weekly Breakdown

### Week 1: Introduction to Consumer Behavior
- Defining consumer behavior and the consumption process.
- Why marketers, policymakers, and consumers themselves study the field.
- Consumption as identity: what our choices signal about us.

### Week 2: Perception
- Sensory systems and hedonic consumption.
- Absolute and differential thresholds (JND) in pricing and packaging.
- Attention, interpretation, and the role of schemas in brand meaning.

### Week 3: Learning and Memory
- Classical and instrumental conditioning applied to brands.
- Observational learning and modeling.
- Encoding, storage, and retrieval; nostalgia and memory for advertising.

### Week 4: Motivation and Affect
- Needs, wants, and motivational conflicts.
- Involvement and its consequences for message processing.
- Emotion, mood, and affect in consumption experiences.

### Week 5: The Self and Identity
- Self-concept, actual vs. ideal self, and self-image congruence.
- Symbolic consumption and the extended self.
- Body image, gender identity, and consumption.

### Week 6: Personality, Lifestyles, and Values
- Trait theories and brand personality.
- Psychographics, lifestyle segmentation, and VALS.
- Core values, materialism, and value-based positioning.

### Week 7: Attitudes and Persuasion
- Attitude structure: the ABC model and multiattribute models.
- Cognitive dissonance and attitude change.
- The Elaboration Likelihood Model; source, message, and audience effects.

### Week 8: Decision Making
- Problem recognition, information search, and evaluation of alternatives.
- Compensatory vs. non-compensatory decision rules.
- Heuristics and biases: anchoring, framing, mental accounting, choice overload.

### Week 9: Buying, Using, and Disposing
- Situational effects: time, physical environment, and antecedent states.
- The shopping experience, in-store decision making, and salesperson influence.
- Post-purchase satisfaction, expectancy disconfirmation, and disposal behavior.

### Week 10: Group Influence and Social Media
- Reference groups, conformity, and normative vs. informational influence.
- Word of mouth, opinion leaders, and influencer marketing.
- Online communities, social proof, and viral diffusion.

### Week 11: Income, Social Class, and Subcultures
- Income, spending patterns, and consumer confidence.
- Social class, status symbols, and taste.
- Age, ethnic, and regional subcultures as consumption communities.

### Week 12: Culture and Consumer Ethics
- Cultural values, rituals, myths, and sacred vs. profane consumption.
- Cross-cultural differences and global consumer culture.
- The dark side: compulsive consumption, addiction, and marketing ethics.
