# Consumer Behavior - Course Curriculum

**Subtitle**: Human and AI-Mediated Consumer Choice  
**Course Level**: Undergraduate  
**Program**: Marketing Major  
**Duration**: 12 Weeks  
**Instructor**: Davood Wadi, PhD  
**Summary**: Why people buy, use, and discard products, and the role of AI agents in consumer choice. Perception, learning and memory, motivation, attitudes, decision-making, heuristics, and delegation to AI agents.  
**Overview**: This course explores why people buy, use, and stop using goods and services, and how artificial intelligence is changing the way they do so. Students examine the internal psychological processes and external social influences that shape consumer choices, together with a new actor in the marketplace: the AI agent that searches, evaluates, and purchases on the consumer's behalf. The curriculum bridges core behavioral theories with everyday marketing decisions and extends these theories to markets in which consumers delegate decisions to AI agents.

---

## Weekly Breakdown

### Week 1: Introduction to Consumer Behavior
**Summary**: The three stages of consumption and the limits of the rational consumer model. The AI agent as a surrogate consumer, and the ethics of influencing consumers and their agents.

- What consumer behavior is and why it matters for marketers.
- The consumer journey: prepurchase, purchase, and postpurchase stages.
- Consumers as individuals versus organizational buyers.
- The AI agent as a surrogate consumer: delegation of search, evaluation, and purchase.
- Marketing ethics, consumer well-being, and dark patterns directed at consumers and at their AI agents.

### Week 2: Perception and Sensory Marketing
**Summary**: Exposure, attention, and interpretation; sensory marketing, sensory thresholds, and Weber's law. Machine perception: product information as structured data, and position effects for consumers and AI agents.

- The perception process: exposure, attention, and interpretation.
- Sensory marketing: how vision, sound, smell, touch, and taste guide buying.
- Sensory thresholds and Weber's Law in brand package changes.
- Perceptual defense, selective attention, and perceptual vigilance.
- Machine perception: product information as structured data, and position effects in the attention of consumers and AI agents.

### Week 3: Learning and Memory
**Summary**: Classical and operant conditioning, observational learning, and the three memory stores. Associative networks, retrieval cues, and brand retrieval when an AI assistant generates the consideration set.

- Behavioral learning theories: classical and operant conditioning.
- Cognitive learning and observational learning in marketing campaigns.
- The memory system: sensory memory, short-term memory, and long-term memory.
- Brand equity, associative networks, and brand retrieval cues.
- Brand retrieval in conversational search: the consideration set generated by an AI assistant.

### Week 4: Motivation, Needs, and Values
**Summary**: Drive and expectancy theories, Maslow's hierarchy, and motivational conflicts. Intrinsic and extrinsic motives in online review writing, and consumer involvement in the delegation of purchases to AI agents.

- Motivation as a psychological drive: drive theory and expectancy theory.
- Distinguishing needs from wants; Maslow's hierarchy of needs in consumer markets.
- Motivational conflicts: approach-approach, approach-avoidance, and avoidance-avoidance.
- Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation: the crowding-out of altruistic motives by financial incentives in online review writing.
- Consumer involvement: product, message, and purchase situation involvement; involvement and the delegation of purchases to AI agents.

### Week 5: Personality, Self-Concept, and Lifestyles
**Summary**: The actual and ideal self, the extended self, brand personality, and psychographics. Resistance to the automation of identity-relevant tasks, and large language models as simulated survey respondents.

- The self-concept: actual self versus ideal self.
- Extended self and the products we use to define our identity.
- Identity-based consumption and consumer resistance to the automation of identity-relevant tasks.
- Personality traits and brand personality dimensions.
- Psychographics and lifestyles: measuring activities, interests, and opinions (AIOs).
- Large language models as simulated respondents in consumer research: synthetic consumers and their limits.

### Week 6: Attitudes and Persuasion
**Summary**: The ABC model, attitude functions, formation hierarchies, source effects, and message framing. Algorithm aversion and algorithm appreciation, and the measurement of AI models' attitudes with consumer scales.

- The nature and functions of consumer attitudes.
- The ABC model of attitudes: affect, behavior, and cognition.
- Attitude formation hierarchies: standard learning, low-involvement, and experiential.
- Persuasion and changing attitudes: source credibility, source attractiveness, and message framing.
- AI as a message source: algorithm aversion and algorithm appreciation.
- Measuring the attitudes of AI models with consumer attitude scales.

### Week 7: Consumer Decision-Making Process
**Summary**: The five-stage decision process, levels of problem solving, information search, and decision rules. Search delegated to AI agents, and the effect of goal specificity in the delegation instruction.

- The five-stage rational decision process: problem recognition to postpurchase evaluation.
- Levels of consumer decision making: routine response, limited problem solving, and extended problem solving.
- Information search strategies: internal search, external search, and search delegated to AI agents.
- The delegation instruction: goal specificity and its effect on the AI agent's information acquisition and choice.
- Evaluating options: evoked set, consideration set, and decision rules.

### Week 8: Behavioral Economics, Heuristics, and Biases
**Summary**: System 1 and System 2, heuristics, cognitive biases, and choice architecture. Heuristic-like choice in AI shopping agents, and information acquisition costs in agent-mediated purchasing.

- System 1 and System 2 thinking in consumer choices.
- Mental shortcuts (heuristics): availability, representativeness, and price-quality inferences.
- Cognitive biases: anchoring, loss aversion, status quo bias, and framing effects.
- Choice architecture, defaults, and nudge theory in consumer environments.
- Heuristic-like choice in AI agents: anchoring, just-below pricing, promotional framing, and position effects.
- Information acquisition costs and storefront information architecture in agent-mediated purchasing.

### Week 9: Post-Purchase Behavior, Satisfaction, and Loyalty
**Summary**: Satisfaction models, cognitive dissonance, product disposal, and loyalty. Online review writing as postpurchase behavior, and loyalty to the brand versus reliance on the AI agent.

- The gap between expectation and reality: customer satisfaction models.
- Cognitive dissonance and buyer's regret.
- Post-purchase dissonance reduction strategies for marketers.
- Product disposal, secondary markets, and the transition to customer loyalty.
- Online review writing as postpurchase behavior: motives, solicitation messages, and incentives.
- Brand loyalty and delegated repurchase: loyalty to the brand versus reliance on the AI agent.

### Week 10: Social Influences, Reference Groups, and Word of Mouth
**Summary**: Reference groups, social power, word of mouth, and opinion leadership. AI-generated review summaries, synthetic reviews, virtual influencers, and the expert power attributed to AI assistants.

- Reference groups: membership, aspirational, and dissociative groups.
- Social power: referent, legitimate, expert, reward, and coercive power; the expert power attributed to AI assistants.
- Word-of-mouth (WOM) communication and opinion leadership; AI-generated review summaries and synthetic reviews.
- Influencer marketing, virtual influencers, and online communities as social proof.

### Week 11: Culture, Subcultures, and Social Class
**Summary**: Cultural values, rituals, subcultures, and social class as a source of status signals. Cultural bias in AI models, including country-of-origin preferences in AI recommendations.

- Culture as the lens through which consumers view products.
- Cultural values, rituals, myths, and sacred versus profane consumption.
- Subcultures based on age, generation, geography, and ethnicity.
- Social class, status symbols, conspicuous consumption, and income inequality.
- Cultural bias in AI models: country-of-origin preferences and cultural representation in AI recommendations.

### Week 12: AI-Mediated Consumption and Future Trends
**Summary**: Mobile shopping, social commerce, and agentic commerce from recommendation to autonomous purchase. Conflicts of duty in AI agents, data ethics, algorithmic pricing, and sustainable consumption.

- The digital consumer: mobile shopping, social commerce, and algorithm-driven discovery.
- Agentic commerce: from algorithmic recommendation to autonomous purchase by AI agents.
- Conflicts of duty in AI agents: sponsored listings, platform delegation, and the consumer as principal.
- Ethical issues in data collection, surveillance, and algorithmic pricing.
- Sustainable consumption and the rise of the conscious consumer.
