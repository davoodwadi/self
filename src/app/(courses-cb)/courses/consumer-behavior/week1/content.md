## Title Slide
- Week 01
- People do not buy products. They buy solutions to feelings, problems, and social needs.

## The Tip of the Iceberg [no-exercise]
- Most people think consumer behavior is just shopping.
- The purchase at the register is only one second in a long chain.
- People spend weeks noticing problems, searching options, and asking friends.
- After the sale, they use the item, feel happy or regretful, and tell others.
- Consumer behavior studies the whole chain: before, during, and after the sale.

## The Three Stages of Consumption [exercise]
[figure: ConsumptionCycle — three stages: Prepurchase, Purchase, and Postpurchase with consumer and marketer views]
- Consumption happens in three distinct stages.
- Stage 1 is Prepurchase. The consumer identifies a need and searches for information. The marketer studies how consumer attitudes form.
- Stage 2 is Purchase. The consumer experiences the store, website, and checkout. The marketer focuses on packaging, convenience, and store atmosphere.
- Stage 3 is Postpurchase. The consumer uses the product and judges its quality. The marketer monitors satisfaction, returns, and repeat purchases.

## Consumers Versus Customers
- In common speech, people use consumer and customer to mean the same thing.
- A customer buys the product. A consumer uses the product.
- A parent buys baby food at the grocery store. The parent is the customer. The baby is the consumer.
- Marketers must satisfy both people. The parent cares about price and nutrition. The baby cares about taste and texture.

## Why Consumers Are Not Calculators [exercise]
- Old economics assumed buyers were perfectly rational.
- This model assumed buyers compare all choices, calculate costs, and pick the best option.
- Real people have limited time and energy. They use mental shortcuts instead.
- Real decisions depend on mood, habits, brand loyalty, and social pressure.
- Marketers who treat consumers as cold calculators make bad predictions.

## Needs Versus Wants
[figure: NeedWantTrio — basic physical/psychological needs branching into cultural wants and commercial demand]
- A need is a basic biological or psychological requirement.
- Examples include thirst, hunger, shelter, and belonging. Needs exist before marketing.
- A want is a specific way to satisfy that need. Culture and personality shape wants.
- A thirsty person in Montreal wants cold tap water or a soda. A thirsty person in another country might want hot tea.
- Marketers do not create basic needs. Marketers create attractive wants to satisfy existing needs.

## The Many Roles One Person Plays
- One purchase often involves several distinct roles.
- The initiator identifies the problem first.
- The influencer gives advice and sways the choice.
- The buyer pays for the goods.
- The user consumes or handles the product.
- In a household buying a family car, children influence the choice, but parents buy the vehicle.

## Market Segmentation: Who Are We Talking To? [exercise]
[figure: SegmentationPillars — geographic, demographic, psychographic, and behavioral pillars]
- You cannot appeal to everyone with the same message.
- Market segmentation divides a big market into smaller groups with shared traits.
- Demographics group people by age, gender, income, and education.
- Geographics group people by location, city size, and climate.
- Psychographics group people by lifestyle, values, and personality.
- Behavioral segments group people by how often they buy and how they use the item.

## The Ethical Border: Helping Versus Manipulating
- Understanding consumers gives firms great power over everyday choices.
- Ethical marketing informs buyers and delivers genuine value.
- Manipulative marketing takes unfair advantage of human cognitive weaknesses.
- Dark patterns are deceptive website designs that trick shoppers into signing up or spending more.
- Regulators and consumers increasingly punish companies that rely on trickery.

## Discussion: Your Last Regretful Purchase
- Discussion: Think of an item you bought recently that you now regret buying. Which stage of consumption failed you? Did you buy it for a need or an impulsive want?
