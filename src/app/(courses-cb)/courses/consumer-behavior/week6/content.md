## Title Slide
- Week 06
- Consumers do not just evaluate products. They feel, think, and act toward them.

## What Are Consumer Attitudes? [no-exercise]
- An attitude is a lasting evaluation of a person, object, or idea.
- Attitudes can be favorable, unfavorable, or somewhere between the two.
- They help consumers simplify repeated choices.
- They can also make consumers resist new information.

## The ABC Model: Feeling, Doing, and Thinking [exercise]
- Affect means feelings and emotions toward an object.
- Behavior means actions or intentions, such as buying, recommending, or avoiding a brand.
- Cognition means beliefs and thoughts about an object.
- The three parts often support each other, but they can also conflict.

## Four Functions of Attitudes [exercise]
- The utilitarian function helps consumers gain benefits or avoid costs.
- The value-expressive function communicates identity, values, and self-image.
- The ego-defensive function protects self-esteem from uncomfortable threats.
- The knowledge function organizes information and makes the world easier to understand.

## How Attitudes Form: Three Hierarchies [exercise]
- The standard learning hierarchy is think, feel, then act.
- The low-involvement hierarchy is think briefly, act, then develop a feeling.
- The experiential hierarchy is feel, act, then explain.
- The hierarchy depends on the product, the consumer, and the purchase situation.

## Who Delivers the Message Changes Its Impact [exercise]
- Source credibility comes from expertise and trustworthiness.
- Expertise makes a source seem knowledgeable.
- Trustworthiness makes a source seem honest and dependable.
- Source attractiveness comes from familiarity, likability, and similarity.

## AI as a Message Source: Aversion and Appreciation [exercise]
- Consumers now receive advice from AI systems as well as from people.
- Algorithm aversion is the tendency to trust an algorithm less than a person. After people see an algorithm make a mistake, they lose confidence in it faster than in a person who makes the same mistake (Dietvorst, Simmons, & Massey, 2015).
- Algorithm appreciation is the opposite tendency. In some tasks, such as numerical estimates, people follow advice more when they are told it comes from an algorithm than from a person (Logg, Minson, & Moore, 2019).
- The task matters. Consumers trust algorithms more for tasks that seem objective, such as calculating a loan payment, than for tasks that seem subjective, such as choosing a gift (Castelo, Bos, & Lehmann, 2019).
- Credibility still rests on expertise and trustworthiness. An AI source can seem expert but not trustworthy when consumers do not know whose interests it serves.

## Framing Changes What the Audience Notices [exercise]
- A positive frame emphasizes gains, benefits, or a desirable result.
- A negative frame emphasizes losses, risks, or the problem that may follow inaction.
- Effective framing depends on the audience, the goal, and whether the choice feels like a gain or a loss.

## Measuring the Attitudes of AI Models [exercise]
- Researchers measure consumer attitudes with rating scales. A Likert scale asks how much a person agrees with a statement, for example from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree).
- Researchers now give the same scales to AI models, because models that advise and shop for consumers may carry attitudes of their own.
- A model does not give one fixed answer. Asked the same question many times, it can give a different rating each time (Wadi & Fredette, 2025).
- A single answer can therefore mislead. Reliable measurement asks the question many times, or reads the probability the model assigns to each point on the scale (Wadi, Ghodrat, & Philp, 2026).
- Measured this way, models from different developers held different attitudes on the same consumer attitude scale (Wadi, Ghodrat, & Philp, 2026).
- An attitude in a model is a pattern in its answers. It is not a feeling that the model experiences.

## Discussion: Audit a Persuasive Message
- Choose an advertisement or product recommendation.
- Identify its affect, behavior, and cognition elements.
- Identify the source and the message frame.
- Discuss whether a different audience would be persuaded by the same message.
